# T-061 review: D-212 elevon linkage (horn hole over the hinge, rod raised, Z-bend jog)

Reviewer: independent review agent, 2026-11-18. Scope: the uncommitted working tree on `redesign/v1` over `164c815a`
(`git diff`), parents R-CAD-02, R-PHY-03, R-PHY-05, R-OPT-01, R-RULE-09. Nothing other than this file was edited.
Nothing was committed.

**Verdict: REJECT as it stands. One Critical finding; the fix is small (see F-1).** The reported numbers reproduce
exactly. But the models that produce them treat the rod end at the arm as a joint fixed at the rod-axis height
(`linkage_core.arm_pt` puts z = `ROD_Z`). The real D-212 joint is a 4.5 mm cranked leg sliding in a clearance hole.
That joint has a free degree of freedom: under compression the rod drops back onto the arm. This gives about 1.4°
of elevon free play and brings back the rod-on-arm contact that D-212 says it removed.

## Findings

### F-1 Critical: the jogged Z-bend is not the joint the models analyse. Under compression the rod drops 3.9 mm onto the arm and the elevon moves 1.4° (free play)
**Evidence.**
- `linkage_core.py:42-43` fixes the rod end at the arm as `arm_pt = (…, ROD_Z)`, a joint at the rod-axis height.
  Physically the arm holds the leg only in x and y, inside a 2 mm thick hole at z 6.7–8.7 mm. The hole has a 0.15 mm
  radial clearance.
- The script's own K5 allowance `ALLOW_Z = atan((1.5 − 1.2)/2) = 8.53°` (`elevon_linkage.py:35`) admits that the leg
  can tilt freely by up to 8.5° in the hole.
- Nothing holds the rod at `ROD_Z`. With the jog, the corner sits 3.9 mm above the arm top. A Z-bend's lower leg stops
  the rod rising, but nothing stops it falling until the corner reaches the arm.
- Moment balance on the rigid crank: the clevis force acts at z 13.2 and the hole reaction at about z 7.7. Under
  compression this couple turns the rod so that the arm end drops, and dropping shortens the pin-to-pin span. So
  compression drives the crank to its stop. Under tension the rod lifts until the lower Z-leg bears on the arm
  underside.
- Rigid-crank solve (scratch `jog.py`; rod hinged at the clevis on the 12.9 mm horn circle, leg ⟂ rod, leg passing
  through the hole at the arm mid-plane, stop when the rod underside at the corner reaches the arm top):
  φ = 5.16° rod tilt and **δ shift = −1.42°** at neutral servo.
- So the linkage has about **1.4° of load-reversal free play** on top of the hole clearance. In the compressed state:
  - the rod rests on the arm again (the condition D-212 lists as fixed: "the rod-to-arm contact is gone");
  - the leg tilts 5.2° ± 1.1° in the hole, up to 6.3°, against the K5 limit of 4.265°. That is the same failure that
    made the agent reject a sloped rod.
- SERVO.md §4 (`redesign/review/QP-4/SERVO.md:35`) sets free play ≤ 1° as the flutter target. The D-205 flutter model
  (`structures/flutter_d205.md`) is linear in K and does not cover free play.

**Required fix.** Hold the rod at its height, or remove the jog. Then rerun AR-001/002/003 with the real joint:
1. Preferred, the common RC practice: a screw-lock pushrod connector (EZ-connector type) in the arm hole, its cross
   hole at rod height, with a straight rod. This gives no jog, no bending in the leg and no vertical freedom. Mass,
   price and height are "-": look them up.
2. A rigid spacer sleeve on the vertical leg, between the arm top and the corner. A tube of about 1.3 mm ID and
   3.9 mm long, bonded or soldered, carries the corner on the arm.
3. Raise the arm with a shim or the real output-boss height so that the arm top is at rod height. `ARM_Z0 = SV_Z1` is
   itself marked "boss height '-': measure".
4. Horn about 9.4 mm over the hinge with an arm hole of about 9 mm. This option was not evaluated; see F-5.

Whichever option is used, model the arm-side joint as it is built: a prismatic-in-z pin or a fixed connector, not a
fixed point at `ROD_Z`. Then repeat the K5 tilt check in the loaded state.

### F-2 Major: the bending in the jogged leg is not bounded by a credible allowable. The material is unknown, and plausible pushrod steels yield or fold at the stall fault
**Evidence.**
- Formula: σ = 32·M/(π·d³), with M = F·e and d = 1.2 mm, so π·d³/32 = 1.696e-10 m³.

  | Case | F | e = rod axis to arm top, 4.50 mm (agent) | e to arm mid-plane, 5.50 mm | e to arm underside, 6.50 mm |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | hold | 12.1 N | 321 MPa | 392 MPa | 463 MPa |
  | stall | 30.2 N | 800 MPa | 978 MPa | 1156 MPa |

  The agent's 321 / 800 MPa takes the least lever. The hole reaction is spread over the 2 mm arm thickness, so 392 /
  978 MPa is the fairer central value.
- Stress concentration at the bend, curved-wire factor (Shigley, hook/torsion-spring form)
  K = (4C² − C − 1)/(4C(C − 1)) with C = 2r/d. For a pliers bend with r ≈ d, K = **1.62**. With it, hold is about
  520–640 MPa and stall about 1300–1600 MPa.
- Music wire, ASTM A228: S_ut = A/d^m with A = 2211 MPa·mm^m and m = 0.145 (Shigley, *Mechanical Engineering
  Design*, Table 10-4) gives **2153 MPa** at 1.2 mm. Bending allowable for torsion springs about 0.78·S_ut =
  **1680 MPa** (Shigley Table 10-6). **These constants are recalled from the textbook and were not re-read for this
  review (no web access in this session). Verify them against the cited table before using them.**
- Result: music wire would survive stall only with K included, at a factor of about 1.05–1.3.
- If the RMRC rod is not music wire (its material is "-", per D-212 itself), the margin can vanish. With an assumed
  S_y of 500 MPa (plain or galvanised steel, assumed, not cited), first yield comes at 18.9 N and a plastic hinge at
  **32 N**, which is the 30.2 N stall-fault force (`jog.py`). The jog would fold.
- Buckling: an eccentric (crooked) column, not Euler on a straight rod.
  - k·L = √(P/EI)·L = 1.64 at 30.2 N. With an end moment P·e at one end, the amplification 1/sin(kL) is about 1.00,
    so the corner moment P·e governs and the 111 N Euler load is not the limit.
  - The limit is collapse of the leg: F_c = M_p/e = S_y·d³/(6e). That is 108 N for music wire, 64 N at S_y 1000 and
    32 N at S_y 500.
  - D3 (peak ≤ Euler/2 = 55.6 N) therefore does not check the governing mode.
- The receipt check "bend test of a rod" (D-212) has no load or pass criterion.

**Required fix.**
- If F-1 option 1, 2 or 3 removes the jog, this finding closes.
- If the jog stays: find the rod material (vendor or a magnet/hardness check), and add a quantitative check. Leg moment
  at F·(e + t_arm/2) × K at the bend, against the cited yield, with a 1.5 factor at the 30 N stall. Add a collapse
  check M_p/e ≥ 1.5 × 30.2 N in place of D3's Euler criterion. Make the receipt test pass/fail: hang 45 N on the
  formed rod, no permanent set.

### F-3 Major: the horn bond peel stress roughly tripled (smaller plate, taller hole) and nothing checks it
**Evidence.**
- The plate went from 20 × 8 to 10 × 8 mm, and the hole from about 9.3 to 13.2 mm above the skin.
- Peak peel on a rigid plate is 6M/(b·L²), with M = F·h:
  - D-212: hold 12.1 N × 13.2 mm, giving **1.20 MPa**; stall fault 30.2 N, giving **2.99 MPa**.
  - D-211: 23.9 N × 9.3 mm on 20 × 8, giving 0.42 MPa.
- The bond is to a tape skin over foam. The nearest check in the repo, `structures/iter3_run.py:196-200`, covers the
  lead-balance block, not this horn. No claim covers the control-horn bond. Foam and tape peel strengths are "-" here.

**Required fix.**
- Add a horn-bond check with a cited foam/skin through-thickness or peel strength.
- If it is short: lengthen the plate (it can extend aft; only the head needs to overhang), add a backing plate with
  through-bolts on the lower skin (usual practice on foam surfaces), or lower the hole height (see F-1 options 3 and 4).

### F-4 Major (R-CAD-02 fidelity): the drawn horn and Z-bend are not real, buildable parts
**Evidence.**
- `horn_blade_loop` invents a custom outline: a 5 mm stem with a round head on a neck that overhangs the hinge line by
  2.5 + 2.2 mm.
- The vendor horn (RMRC M02) publishes only "20 mm height". The build text says "choose or drill the hole". A stock
  horn whose base runs under its hole cannot put the hole over the hinge line without bonding across the hinge gap.
  So this shape is either a modified or printed horn (no BOM line, no drawing, mass "1.0 g EST" unchanged) or it
  cannot be built.
- CLAUDE.md requires every part modelled to its real shape, and unknown dimensions marked, never invented.
- The CAD Z-bend has no lower retaining leg. The pin is "flush with the arm underside", and the arm sits on the case
  top with zero gap (`ARM_Z0 = SV_Z1`), so there is no room for one.
- The build guide lists "Z-bend pliers" for D4. Such pliers form a fixed short offset (from general practice, not
  looked up). The 6.5 mm leg (4.5 mm jog + 2 mm arm) has to be hand-bent, and the build guide gives no jog dimension.

**Required fix.**
- Name the actual horn (or make it a printed part with a drawing, mass and BOM line), and draw its real outline.
- Model the full Z-bend with its lower leg, which needs the real boss gap, or the connector chosen in F-1.
- Put the forming dimensions in BUILD D4.

### F-5 Major (R-OPT-01 ladder): lighter and simpler options were not traded; the 9 mm rejection is right only for a 12 mm arm
**Evidence.**
- CL-A-SERVO-COLD (`proofs/claims_a2_struct.py:177-191`) computes factor = stall·(horn/arm)/(1.5·(hinge + preload +
  damper_cold)). It assumes gearing = arm/horn = 0.930.
- After D-212 the true gearing at ±20° is 0.904–0.934. The assumption now holds: within 0.4 % on the −20° side, and
  conservative on the +20° side. CHAIN.md 1.582 times 0.930/0.934 gives 1.575 ≥ 1.5. On D-211 it was false (gearing
  1.91, factor about 0.77, trade table).
- So rejecting "hole 9.4 mm over the hinge with the 12 mm arm" is correct: 1.582 × (9.4/12)/(12.9/12) = 1.15 < 1.5.
  The agent quotes 1.12.
- Options missing from the trade table (`docs/engineering-decisions.md`, D-212):
  1. Horn 9.4 mm with an arm hole of about 9 mm. Claim 1.582 × (9.4/9)/(12.9/12) = **1.54**; hold margin about 2.8;
     standard Z-bend at arm height, no jog. Arm hole row "-".
  2. A pushrod connector or spacer at rod height (F-1).
  3. An arm shim, or the measured boss height.
  4. A sloped straight rod (5.15° at neutral). Rejected implicitly by K5, but the D-212 jog ends in the same slope when
     compressed (F-1), so that rejection was not sound.

  Each of these keeps the horn perpendicular and avoids the bending in F-2.
- The ladder text also calls rung 5 (stronger servo) "adds mass and cost", but the trade table leaves both "-".

**Required fix.** Add rows for options 1–3 (at least the connector and shim) with mass, cost, margin and evidence, and
choose again. Log the outcome in a revised D-212 or in D-213.
