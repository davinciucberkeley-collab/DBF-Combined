## 1. Q1: what I recomputed

### 1.1 Where each number comes from (code checked)
- **1.61** = `DAF_Mexit_env` = max over runs of (max over cases of the dynamic sleeve-exit moment) / (max over cases of the **sim rigid-body** exit moment) (`t076d_step3_d1.py` l.90; RB from `landing_a.simulate` foot forces through `T2.wrench_split_pts`, l.25-29, l.78-80). Recomputed from `step3_d1.json` `overall`: 1.6133, run `0.02|hi|0.01` (8.45 / 5.24). **Reproduced.**
- **3.08** = `DAF_Mexit_casemax` = max over cases with RB >= 0.5 x RB envelope of dyn / RB (l.84, l.90). Recomputed: 3.0799, run `0.02|kd|0.01`, case **"SITL Lb_5e narrow (z drift)"**: dynamic 8.22 N m (the dynamic envelope of that run), RB 2.67 N m = **51 %** of the RB envelope 5.24. That is 1 % above the cut-off. **Reproduced.**
- **The same-model reference exists in the same file.** The `rig` tag (`run_case(..., rigid_only=True)`, `t076c_step2_d1.py` l.118-122) is the FE frame's quasi-static response: rigid modes integrated, flexible modes q = g / omega^2. This is the textbook static reference of a DAF (mode-acceleration). T-076d computed it but used it only for the "QS/RB arm Mz 0.78" consistency line (s3 bullet 3).

### 1.2 Why the case-max (3.08) is not a valid scale
- A DAF is a scale only if multiplying the static demand by it bounds the dynamic demand of the **same** case set. The case-max ratio is valid for its own case (2.67 x 3.08 = 8.22). Applied to the enveloped RB set, which is what the FE pass lines do (RB envelope 5.24 x 3.08 = **16.1 N m**), it predicts a moment that no case reaches. The largest dynamic exit moment over all 18 runs is 8.45 N m, so 16.1 is **1.91 x** that (hand: 16.14 / 8.45).
- The value depends on the cut-off. At a 44 % cut-off, "hard 10 narrow" (RB 2.32, dyn 7.59) gives **3.27**. The 50 % threshold has no physical basis.
- The dynamic peaks come from **narrow** cases; the RB envelope comes from **wide** cases (top-ratio list, run `0.02|kd|0.01`: 6 of the top 8 ratios are narrow cases). A ratio between two different governing cases is not an amplification of either.
- So rejecting 3.08 is not a loosening. 3.08 was never adopted (T-076d judged at 1.61 and flagged 3.08 for a ruling; T-076c used 1.5). It does not describe any physical demand. **The stricter correct reading is the same-model envelope, s1.3.**

### 1.3 The same-model DAF (envelope, max over the 18 runs; EST: one-way coupling, modal damping 1 % EST)

| quantity (arm group unless named) | dyn / FE quasi-static, envelope | dyn / sim RB, envelope (T-076d) | FE quasi-static / sim RB (r_m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| sleeve-exit moment Mexit | **1.93** | 1.61 | 0.84 (every run) |
| arm Mz | 1.94 | 1.44 (T-076d s3 bullet 4) | 0.81 |
| beam sigma at s 25 mm | **1.97** | 1.65 (97.1 / 58.8) | 0.84 |
| beam tau (RF >= 5, not binding) | 2.41 | — | — |
| bridge \|(My, Mz)\| at the mouth | **2.23** | — | — |
| hub-root Mz | 1.96 | — | — |
| arm-wrench forces Fx / Fy / Fz | 2.25 / 3.46 / 5.22 | — | — |

Per zeta (Mexit, 1 % damping, kd / nom / hi): z0.02 1.88 / 1.81 / 1.93; z0.05 1.66 / 1.61 / 1.81; z0.10 1.43 / 1.29 / 1.38. At 2 % damping it is 15-20 % lower. **Why it is the correct reading:** the pass lines multiply the sim RB wrench (partition B, through the stub ends of the hub FE) by a scalar. That scalar must turn the static demand into the dynamic demand of the model the static demand came from. The dyn / sim-RB ratio (1.61) also contains the mass-model difference: the FE frame gives only 0.84 of the sim's static arm-group moment. Dividing by the larger sim value lowers the ratio by that 0.84. That **under-states** the amplification whenever the sim's CAD-derived mass model is the more correct one. T-076d s1 says the two mass models "bracket each other (EST)", so neither can be assumed right. Without that assumption, the same-model DAF on the sim RB is the consistent and stricter choice. The direct D-1 rows carry the same bias the other way (FE arm group 0.84 of the sim), hence the D-1 / r_m term.
- **Limit of the transfer:** it only holds while r_m is near 1. On the T-076c basis (old ITEMS model) the bridge r_m was about 0.3 (FE quasi-static about 5.7 N m against sim RB 17.5 N m, from T-076c s2: DAF 2.82 against FE-QS, 0.91 against sim RB). There both ratios were meaningless, and T-076c was right not to stop on 2.82 x 17.5. That basis is superseded by the T-076d CAD mass model (r_m 0.81-0.84). **Required:** reconcile the D-1 frame's mass mapping with `landing_mass_cad.py` (seconds per run, an analysis task, not done here). The DAF and r_m then follow directly. If r_m ends up outside 0.8-1.25, stop and report rather than scale.
- **Per-row scalars:** use the quantity that drives the row. Sleeve-exit, tube and bond rows use **1.97** (beam sigma, the largest of Mexit / Mz / sigma). The bridge uses 2.23. Rows on a path D-1 does not output (wing skin, fin, shoe, saddle, root block) have no computed DAF. Until D-1 outputs a wing-root / saddle section, use the largest moment DAF, **2.23**. The 1.5 floor alone would be less than every computed moment DAF at zeta <= 0.05, so it would loosen. Force-driven effects (Fz flapping x5.2) are not covered by any scalar; the D-1 direct / r_m term covers them.

### 1.4 The floor
The x1.5 is an EST placeholder for flexibility, not a safety factor (escalation s2.4). The computed DAF is itself EST for four reasons: one-way coupling with a 1.25 ms contact onset that is a model property (root-cause s1.6), modal damping 1 / 2 % EST (DAF moves 15-20 % between them), an unmeasured pad law, and r_m 0.84. Keep the floor. Use 1 % damping until TP-10 measures the modal damping (the tap test can give it). The floor can only be reviewed after three things: TP-3 gives the pad law, D-1 is re-run with it and the reconciled mass, and a drop-test acceleration trace validates D-1. Even then it binds only at zeta >= about 0.09, where the plan already rejects the pad (s0 Q1c), so **I rule that it stays**.

Readings:
- **TP-4:** T-081's 2.9 MPa line is low even under T-076d's own reading. T-076d s4.3 quotes "2.6-2.9 MPa", but its s5 lists the bond floor RF as 0.55 (z0.10), which needs 3.66 MPa. With the correct DAF the bound is 4.5 MPa. The force frame needs **> 5.4 kN** at L 32 plus margin to break the coupon, so the T-081 ">= 5 kN" spec is not enough. The coupon must reproduce the design bond: G1 bonds Al to the CF doubler for s 11-32 and to the tube for |s| < 11 (T-076d s4.1 'full' definition), with the 45 deg mouth chamfer and the build's prep. A bare 12x10 tube in a sleeve tests a different interface. Run it at L 32 only; a mean shear at L 24 does not transfer to L 32 (shear lag). "Min of 5" is acceptable because KP already carries the 1.15 material factor.
- **TP-5:** the FE interlaminar maximum is singular at the taper tip and rises on refinement (T-076d s4.4), so **no MPa pass line from this mesh is meaningful**, whether 43 or 53. The only valid form is a load ratio on the same detail. The coupon must be tube + tapered doubler + chamfered Al sleeve, loaded at the sleeve exit in the design's bending and shear. Pass: failure load >= KF x max(DAF, 1.5) x the design-equivalent load (DAF 1.97 bound), or >= the D-1 / r_m equivalent if that is larger. A plain-tube short-beam test gives a material number that cannot be compared with a singular peak.
- **TP-8:** a line of r >= 1.15 judges the skin at RB with no DAF. That breaks the floor rule (max(DAF, 1.5) applies to every landing row, escalation s2.4). The floor alone already needs r >= 1.72 at the bound. The Hoff-Mautner 0.5 coefficient (T-071) is the usual conservative value, so a measured r of 2.26-2.56 is unlikely (EST, no data). **Plan on the 2-ply fallback (+27 g, T-071) unless TP-3 shows zeta <= 0.05 and D-1 gives a wing DAF.** Even then r about 1.7-1.9 is needed.
- **TP-3's hard line** should be the lowest zeta limit over all rows. Tube B axial now limits it to about 0.088 (EST), just inside the 0.09 from A-P5. Write it as **<= 0.085** (EST, rounded down) until D-1 is re-run.
