### D-210 motor cup 1 mm fillets and web
Sources (read 2026-11-23):
1. Printed Solid, "3D Printing Design Tips - Part 1: Stress Concentrations", https://printedsolid.com/blogs/news/37036739-3d-printing-design-tips-part-1-stress-concentrations (page undated; copyright year 2026). Supports: sharp corners concentrate stress in printed parts and fillets reduce it, because printing does not round corners the way machining does.
2. Kilic, Madenci, Ambur, "Global-local finite element analysis of bonded single-lap joints", AIAA / NASA Langley paper, 2004, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20040070719/downloads/20040070719.pdf (PDF text read). Supports: peak peel and shear stresses at a bonded joint edge can be reduced by an adhesive overflow (fillet) at the edge. Applies to the cup-to-tube bond edge, not to the printed sleeve-to-plate corner.
Not found: a radius-to-step ratio for a printed PETG corner (searched printed-part design guides; Markforged page returned HTTP 403, Stratasys PDF not opened).
Proposed one line: `Benchmark (6.4): Printed Solid "3D Printing Design Tips Part 1: Stress Concentrations" (undated, read 2026-11-23): sharp corners in printed parts concentrate stress and fillets reduce it -> supports 1 mm fillets on the sleeve-to-plate edge; NASA/AIAA Kilic et al. 2004 (read 2026-11-23): a fillet at a bonded edge lowers peak peel/shear -> supports the bond-edge treatment; difference from our case: neither gives a radius for a 1.8 mm PETG wall, so the size is from our FEA (D-210 run).`

### D-212 rev 3 horn attachment (rib horn)

### D-215 landing gear and fin-joint redesign
Sources (read 2026-11-23):
1. Ma, Smeur, de Croon, "Design and Control of a Tilt-Rotor Tailsitter Aircraft With Pivoting VTOL Capability", IEEE RA-L vol. 10 no. 6, June 2025 (TU Delft repository copy, DOI 10.1109/LRA.2025.3563821), https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_b3334c3a-c07d-4dbe-99ee-0313c1d61960 (PDF text read). Supports: conventional tailsitters stand upright on added landing gear that costs weight and drag and can tip over; this is the baseline practice our gear follows.
2. Swart, "Autonomous landing of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles: Eliminating tailsitter VTOL tip overs in high wind scenarios", TU Delft MSc thesis, 26 November 2024, https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_5ffe54c7-e211-46e4-94e9-200a3a8e21b1?preview=1 (PDF text read). Supports: a real tailsitter (Marlyn, 5.87 kg, gear rods) stands on landing rods and tip-over geometry is set by CG height against the rod footprint.
UHMW-PE face on landing pads: no benchmark found. Searched UHMW friction data sheets; results gave values against steel (typically 0.1 to 0.2) in sheet suppliers' tables (search results only; Proto Labs PDF opened but unreadable), none for UHMW on pavement or grass, so nothing supports the mu used; keep "mu is a measured requirement" (T-050 s3).
Proposed one line: `Benchmark (6.4): Ma et al., IEEE RA-L 2025, and Swart, TU Delft thesis 2024 (both read 2026-11-23): upright tailsitters rest on added landing gear (rods) whose footprint sets tip-over; -> supports outboard pads / fin-tip gear; difference from our case: neither treats side load into a fin joint or UHMW pad friction (no benchmark found for UHMW on ground).`

### D-216 wing torsion skin (glass +-45)
Sources (read 2026-11-23):
1. L. Joyner, "New Construction Materials For Gliders", Model Aviation, November 2011 p. 125, https://www.modelaviation.com/node/145. Supports: cloth skins on a foam core act as a stressed skin carrying bending and twist, and cloth laid at 45 deg to the chord markedly raises torsional stiffness.
Searched also: foam-core RC wing sheeting articles (one Model Aviation 2008 article opened: balsa skins only, no glass or bias, not cited).
Proposed one line: `Benchmark (6.4): Joyner, "New Construction Materials For Gliders", Model Aviation Nov 2011 (read 2026-11-23): fibreglass/carbon cloth skins on a foam core carry the twist, and 45 deg cloth raises torsional stiffness -> supports a +-45 deg glass skin over the wing; difference from our case: a hobby glider article, not a landing-torque load case or a quantified allowable (our numbers are T-071 data sheets and FEA).`

### D-224 CG closure by rearrangement
Sources (read 2026-11-23):
1. D. Pappas, "If It Flies", Model Aviation, August 2009 pp. 75-76, https://library.modelaviation.com/article/if-it-flies-200908. Supports: tailless models fly well at about 10 percent static margin, get sluggish above 15-20 percent, and flying wings and deltas can fly with margins as low as about 5 percent.
2. RCU Forums, "Delta static margin", thread of 18 Oct 2005, https://www.rcuniverse.com/forum/aerodynamics/3467431-delta-static-margin-print.html. Supports: delta and low-aspect flying wings are commonly set at 10 to 15 percent static margin, start from 10 percent and tune in flight (forum opinion, weak).
Neither source is the origin of the A-P4 "SM 6 percent flight-evidence limit"; that origin was not found in these sources (state it as repo-derived).
Proposed one line: `Benchmark (6.4): Pappas, Model Aviation Aug 2009 (read 2026-11-23): tailless aircraft tolerate about 10 percent static margin, as low as about 5 percent for flying wings -> brackets our SM target and supports closing CG by moving mass rather than adding ballast; difference from our case: hobby glider guidance for a passive aircraft, not an actively stabilised VTOL (A-P4 6 percent limit is repo-derived).`

### D-226 landing load path through the torsion cell
Sources (read 2026-11-23):
1. J. Schwenke and D. Krause, "Optimization of load introduction points in sandwich structures with additively manufactured cores", Design Science vol. 6, online 1 June 2020 (Hamburg University of Technology), DOI 10.1017/dsj.2020.10, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/design-science/article/optimization-of-load-introduction-points-in-sandwich-structures-with-additively-manufactured-cores/F17A4E995673EFA7F8556E45A69FF90C. Supports: load introduction points are the weak spots of a sandwich panel because a light core cannot take local loads, and inserts or a load-path-shaped core are used to spread them into the skins.
No model-aircraft or UAV text on bulkhead-to-skin torsion-cell load introduction was found (searched sandwich-wing load introduction; handbook results not opened).
Proposed one line: `Benchmark (6.4): Schwenke and Krause, Design Science 2020 (read 2026-11-23): load introduction points are the weak spot of a foam/core sandwich and need an insert or load-path spreading into the facings -> supports taking the landing load through a local node into the torsion skin instead of the bare core; difference from our case: honeycomb panel pull-out tests, not a wing skin cell under landing torque.`

### D-233 longer fin tubes close A-P9
Sources (read 2026-11-23):
1. Swart, TU Delft MSc thesis, 26 Nov 2024, https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_5ffe54c7-e211-46e4-94e9-200a3a8e21b1?preview=1 (PDF text read). Supports: for a real tailsitter the CG alone sets a tip-over limit pitch angle (32.5 deg for the 5.87 kg Marlyn from its gear footprint dimensions), and wind lowers it (47.5 deg at 8.4 m/s); a longer footprint raising the angle is the same geometry as our A-P9.
2. Ma et al., IEEE RA-L 2025 (see D-215): tailsitters with landing gear can tip over and then need manual intervention.
A-P9 (30 deg) itself is not given by either source; it stays repo-derived.
Proposed one line: `Benchmark (6.4): Swart, TU Delft MSc thesis 2024 (read 2026-11-23): tip-over angle of a tailsitter follows from CG height and gear footprint (32.5 deg for Marlyn, less with wind) -> supports widening the footprint with longer fin tubes; difference from our case: Marlyn is a 5.87 kg dual-motor tailsitter with rods; the A-P9 30 deg criterion is DERIVED in the repo, not taken from it.`

### D-239 flight controller 75 mm off centre
Sources (read 2026-11-23):
1. ArduPilot Plane docs, "Mounting the Autopilot", https://ardupilot.org/plane/docs/common-mounting-the-flight-controller.html (page undated, footer 2024). Supports: mount near the CG, but exact centre is not critical; if it cannot be central the IMU position offset parameters can recover some performance; foam pads are the simple damping method.
2. ArduPilot Plane docs, "Vibration Damping", https://ardupilot.org/plane/docs/common-vibration-damping.html (undated, footer 2024). Supports: soft-mount the board, a stiffer frame helps, and measure vibration to see whether more damping is needed.
Proposed one line: `Benchmark (6.4): ArduPilot "Mounting the Autopilot" and "Vibration Damping" (both read 2026-11-23): placement close to the CG is preferred, an offset is allowed with the IMU position offset parameters, and soft mounting with measured vibration governs damping -> supports a 75 mm off-centre FC with INS offsets and foam mount; difference from our case: generic multirotor/plane guidance, wing-mode vibration at the FC is checked by the T-091 candidate sheet, not by these pages.`
