## Mass gate justification

- Total added by D-202..D-210 (from the log): hub +16.7 g (D-202), -1.8 doublers, -5.6 lead (D-205), +2.1 bridge (D-206), +1.3 hub fillets (D-207), +3.3 board/lines/lead (D-208), +0.28 cup EST (D-210). Net basic: 1,261.3 (pre) to 1,273.2 g (D-208); predicted 1.4698 kg against a 1.470 kg gate: **0.2 g margin**.
- FLAG M1: **the design target was raised three times to follow the prediction: 1.46 to 1.465 (D-205), to 1.467 (D-207), to 1.470 (D-208, now equal to the gate).** That removes the target-versus-gate buffer. D-208 says "user accepted". That is acceptable as a user call, but the target has stopped being a control. The prediction still contains 142 g of growth and 48 g of overrun, which is where the real buffer is. Recommend the next ledger update states the target as 1.465 again, or an explicit "target = gate, margin held in growth/overrun" record.
- FLAG M2: **`redesign/qp/A/MASS-GATE.md` is stale.** Its last roll-up table (lines 125-157) is 1,455.6 / 1,458.1 g against "<= 1,460 (target), 1,470 (gate)". Grep finds no D-202..D-210 mention. R-OPT-03 requires the mass roll-up current after each change. Rerun `mission_gate_a.py` / update the file (not done by this review).
- FLAG M3: the 1.47 kg gate itself has little remaining physics room. Earlier MASS-GATE section 1 showed the capped transition window at +-4.5 % at 1.50 kg and +-5 % at 1.486 kg. The 1.47 kg window figure should be re-read in section 8 (not re-checked here).
- Flutter margin: damper-on cases at 31.5 m/s (half-filled) against the 28.8 m/s limit, undamped fails. Any further lead or mass cut moves this. This is a function-first constraint the mass work depends on.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. No decision names ladder rungs in order (T-024 rule is new). D-205, D-206, D-207, D-208 effectively did climb in order (geometry/rearrange before material), which is good. D-202 is the one rung-5 add with the most mass (+16.7 g) and it records the heavier options rejected but not rungs 1-3 on the old hub.
2. No benchmark/literature citation for any hub/joint mechanism (R-OPT-04). Candidates: printed-lug and bonded-sleeve motor-mount designs on Pixhawk-class quad frames, bonded-sleeve joint practice (e.g. Hart-Smith / MIL-HDBK-17 bonded joints). Needs searching and dating by whoever writes TR-1; I do not supply URLs here.
3. Allowables that are EST drive every sizing: 2.0 MPa bond (coupon not yet run, `COUPON-BOND-D202.md`), 30 MPa tube shear, 40 hoop, 350 axial. These sit at the heart of D-202/206/207. The coupon is the blocking item before the flight hub print.
4. D-206 strut-socket bending (about 1.5 N m each if struts take the upper-bound share) has no check. Open, high-consequence, cheap to analyse.

## Lighter / cheaper alternatives that look better (stated with evidence)

- D-206: none cheaper than 16x14 from offcut at +2.1 g and $0. A 12x8 saves 0 and costs +4.9 g plus a purchase. Verdict: keep. Possible lighter route: let the V struts share roll (their share is 1-60 %, unknown); measuring that share could permit 14x12 (fails by 0.4-2 MPa alone, 30.4/32.0 vs 30, so share of about 5-10 % would pass). Evidence: log's own numbers; not verified. Weak, a conditional possibility only.
- D-202: no lighter alternative found in the record. Not exhaustive: a thinner printed hub with carbon cross-plates was not considered.
- D-210: none; 0.28 g total.
