## 2. Root cause

### 2.1 The demand: in-plane roll of the motor group, not pitch
In a wide-axis touchdown (lean or drift along the span), one fin's two feet strike first, 0.38 m out in y. Their x forces (thrust-axis direction, 41-47 N per foot, V8) spin the aircraft about the wing normal (body z): Mz_cg 38-47 N m. The motors and arms (motor group) sit ahead of the hub on two continuous 12x10 tubes. Their rotary inertia about z has to be reacted through the bridge tube, the PETG bore and the block, into the arm sleeves. The rigid-body hub-side share is Mz 17.5 N m: about 2.6 x the largest My (6.71) and 2.9 x the T-071b design moment (6.05). The worst families by |Mz| are:

| Family | max \|Mz\| N m | max \|My\| N m |
|---|---|---|
| hard 10 wide + drift .5 y | 17.51 | 3.58 |
| hard 16 wide + drift .5 y | 16.94 | 3.50 |
| hard 10 wide | 16.68 | 2.67 |
| SITL Tb_h5 wide (y drift) | 14.45 | 3.28 |
| SITL Z5_b45 wide (y drift) | 13.40 | 3.07 |
| hard 7/7 combo | 12.34 | 4.93 |

Even the SITL-envelope wide cases (12.1-14.5 N m) are about 2 x the design moment. Removing the drift does not help either: "hard 10 wide" with no drift gives 16.7. The pitch torque (My) governs the wing torsion skin (T-070), not the hub. T-076 F-2 saw Mz 17.6 but treated it only as a bridge-bending note, and T-076b then tuned bore depth against a moment that was not the governing one.

### 2.2 Why no bore or collar geometry can close it
The bore bearing (V3) could be fixed by length alone: L >= 26.6 / 32.6 mm. But the full Mz must also cross the block between the floor and the sleeves. That section is at most 20 x 20 mm of PETG, with a capacity of 12.4 N m solid (V4), against 17.5 at RB. This is the "block forward of the floor band" region in T-076b (38 % of over-allowable points), and it is why the whole-hub p99 stays at 1.05-1.08 even with the floor band removed. **V2 will fail by hand calc (bearing 1.13 but net section 0.71). Do not run it.** Widening the block to the 27 mm the raceways allow gives a solid RF of 1.29 at RB and 0.86 at x1.5, before the holes are subtracted.

### 2.3 Material, allowable, print orientation
The part is Prusament PETG (R03). The FEA allowable is 20 MPa isotropic EST, and the interlayer value is 9.5 MPa EST. For Mz-dominated bending the governing sigma_xx lies in the x-y plane, so only a **z-up print (layers parallel to x-y)** earns the 20 MPa. No hub drawing fixes this. For any PETG structural part in this load path the print orientation is load-bearing and must be on the drawing (T-076 already found the same for R4).

### 2.4 Acceptance definition (where set, what is wrong, what is correct; nothing loosened)
- **Where set:** D-207 (CHANGE-LOG: sharp corners are singular, "so judged by the 99th percentile", for flight and strike cases); T-076 s1.1 extended it to landing as "zone p99 (D-207)" plus a stricter whole-hub p99; coded in `t076_main.py` `eval_land`.
- **What is wrong:** (a) The percentile is over Gauss points, not volume, so it depends on the mesh-density map. Refinement boxes put many points at hot spots; adding a large low-stress region (the collar, 233,940 points) dilutes it (V9). (b) It is pooled over the whole part, so the 1 % allowed over-stress can sit entirely in the one section the load must pass through (here 46 % floor band and 38 % block forward). In a printed part that is a crack path, not an artefact. (c) It checks the first principal stress against an isotropic in-layer allowable with no print orientation (2.3). (d) The tied bridge end face carries tension (T-076b b.3); the real end is contact or an epoxy end bond (2.0 MPa EST).
- **What is correct (proposal for the conductor; equal or stricter everywhere):** For each load-path zone (bore wall, floor/web, block net section between bore and sleeves, sleeves, collar): a **volume-weighted** p99.9 of the governing stress, with only a fixed physical distance (0.5 mm EST) from tied re-entrant corners excluded, and a mesh-convergence check (< 5 % change on one refinement). For PETG, split the stress into layer-normal (against 9.5 MPa) and in-layer (against 20 MPa) for the declared print orientation; for metal, von Mises against the cited yield with KF 1.725. Always add the **hand net-section and bearing rows** (V3, V4): FEA is never the only evidence. Model the end face as released (gap) and judge bonds by mean shear (the D-209 bond rule). Under this rule the PETG hub fails on more rows, not fewer, so the definition error is real but it is not the way out.
- **x1.5:** set in T-071b as an EST placeholder for flexibility (`landing_centre_a.py` l.94). It is a dynamic amplification on the rigid-body demand, not a safety factor (KP covers ultimate, material and print). Replace it with a computed DAF (run D-1, s3.3); never drop it. Until D-1 exists, judge every row at max(DAF from D-1, 1.5).

### 2.5 Load path
Topology: wing to saddle to bridge to hub to arm tubes. This is the only Mz path today (V10), and moving it elsewhere costs more than it saves (s3, rows 3b and 5b). The error is putting a CF-tube-to-CF-tube moment connection of 17.5 N m x DAF into a 20 x 20 mm printed block crossed by two 12 mm tube holes.

## 4. SITL pitch-use item

### 4.1 Mechanism
- `A.param` l.74-82 (QP-4, D-186): THR_MAX 68 with `FWD_BAT_VOLT_MAX 16.8`, `FWD_BAT_VOLT_MIN 13.2`, `FWD_BAT_IDX 0`. - ArduPilot parameter text, local source copy (`C:/Users/dillo/tools/ardupilot-sitl/src/Parameters.cpp` l.1141-1165, commit bf693f31, Plane 4.7.3-beta1, read 2026-11-20): "Forward throttle battery voltage compensation maximum voltage (voltage above this will have no additional scaling effect on thrust) ... 0 = Disabled. Recommend THR_MAX is set to no more than 100 x FWD_BAT_VOLT_MIN / FWD_BAT_VOLT_MAX ...". With 16.8 / 13.2 that recommended maximum is 78.6, and 68 meets it. **The function body (ArduPlane `servos.cpp`, throttle voltage compensation) is not on disk and was not read today.** The ceiling arithmetic is the A.param record and the parameter description, not the code.

### 4.2 Is the limit really reached?
Yes for this case, as designed. The compensation is what makes the sagged pack reach the fresh-pack ceiling thrust: same terminal voltage, 0.766 x 14.92 = 0.68 x 16.8 = 11.4 V. The 0.61-0.63 is therefore the aircraft that A.param describes, and T-074's 0.44-0.47 was an under-thrust aircraft (V_end 16.4 against 17 m/s cruise). The configuration closest to flight is r2 (monitor on, **both** FWD_BAT and Q_M_BAT_VOLT live, as in A.param): **0.59**, inside the 0.60 line by 0.01, with the run noise at 0.03 (T-076b). That is marginal and not a shown pass. T-076's reason for switching Q_M_BAT_VOLT off ("the plant has none") is wrong: the plant's thrust depends on its bus voltage (`a_plant.py` l.41 VREST, l.131-153: bus voltage = rest voltage less pack-resistance sag), which is exactly what the FC compensation corrects. r2 also changes the transition: handover 14.6 m/s, altitude gain 43.9 m, u_max 0.887, pack 32.8 A. **The C3 / T-074 transition matrix must be re-run in the flight battery configuration.** That is a finding against the harness, not against the aircraft.
