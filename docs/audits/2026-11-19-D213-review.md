## 2. Findings

### F1 CRITICAL: the real load path is not checked; three of the five "part capacity" rows describe geometry A does not have
Evidence:
- The CAD foot load path is: EVA/PE pad 12 x 15 x 30 mm (GER-002, stuck with PSA or double-sided tape, `a_build_steps.py` C9 l.499-506) -> printed PETG shoe (GER-001: 12 mm ring on the fin-tube end, cap and a 5.5 x 21 x 15 mm plate, `cad/amodel/electrical.py` l.1068-1074) -> **16x14 CF fin tube** (FIN-001, `wing.py` l.689) -> **printed PETG root block** (FIN-003, 3.2 g: 60 mm ring on the fin tube plus a 19 x 19 mm socket over x 0.255-0.286, i.e. 31 mm rib engagement, `wing.py` l.694-696) -> 14x12 rib (FIN-002) -> **printed PETG saddle** (FIN-005, 1.0 g: 24 mm socket over x 0.0665-0.0905, i.e. 20 mm rib engagement, `wing.py` l.704-707) -> 12x10 spar.
- `landing_side_a.py` s1 rows "shoe bond on fin corner 1,500 mm2", "foam bearing at the fin slot 65 x 23 mm" and "fin skin 0.1 mm CFRP bending" use `structures_a.py` s6 geometry. D-214 (`CHANGE-LOG.md` l.922) states `structures_a.py` is the QP-3 iteration-1 baseline geometry, "not the current design". The A fin is a CF tube with an XPS fairing; it has no 0.1 mm CFRP skins, no fin-corner foam bond and no 65 x 23 mm fin slot in the load path. The `structures_a.py` s6 rows for the root block ("60 mm embed", "60 x 40 mm both faces") and the rib saddle ("wrap 16 x 30 mm") also do not match the CAD (31 mm, 20 mm and a 60 mm ring on a 12 mm spar).
- The PETG parts at both ends of the rib carry the same torsion (Mx up to 3.29 N m limit) and bending (up to 10.3 N m at the root, R3 `max|Mbend|`) as the rib, with the printed factor 1.25 (KP 2.156, `structures_a.py` l.11-12) and allowables two orders of magnitude lower (PETG_BEAR 20 MPa, PETG_ILAY 9.5 MPa, BOND_TAU 2.0 MPa, `structures_a.py` l.16-17). Reviewer hand estimates, all **EST, indicative only** (rigid socket, no load sharing with the foam groove, which the guest list in `wing.py` l.673 shows the rib is let into):
  - Saddle socket, 20 mm engagement, bending 10.3 N m x 2.156 = 22.2 N m: socket-end bearing 6M/(d L^2) = 6 x 22.2 / (0.014 x 0.020^2) = 23.8 MPa vs PETG_BEAR 20 MPa: **RF about 0.84**. Torsion 3.29 x 2.156 = 7.1 N m on the bond, tau = T/(2 pi r^2 L) = 1.15 MPa vs 2.0 MPa: RF 1.7.
  - Saddle socket wall as a section (19 x 19 mm box, 14 mm bore, Z about 945 mm3): 22.2 N m / 945 mm3 = 23.5 MPa vs PETG_ILAY 9.5 MPa: **RF about 0.4** if the layers lie across the socket (print orientation not stated).
  - Pad to shoe plate (PSA, 15 x 12 = 180 mm2): side 18.7 N x 2.156 / 180 mm2 = 224 kPa shear, plus peel from the 30 mm pad column moment. The project's own foam-bond value (0.1 MPa, the one D-213 uses for the shoe bond) gives **RF about 0.45**; no PSA or EVA shear allowable is sourced ("-").
  - Root block socket, 31 mm: torsion bond 0.74 MPa (RF about 2.7), bearing from My 5.3 N m x 2.156 about 5.1 MPa (RF about 3.9). Fin tube 16x14 bending at the root-block edge (46.7 N x 0.147 m with the side component): about 72 MPa design vs 350 MPa, RF about 4.6. These look adequate.
- Conclusion: the statements "the failing/limiting part is the rib root" and "nothing in A reaches 1.0 at the worst simulated load" (`T064_landing_side.md` s3, AR-012 Result, D-213 (3)) are not supported. The weakest parts are more likely the printed saddle and the pad attachment, which were not checked on their real geometry.
Required fix: check every part on the CAD load path (pad and its PSA, shoe plate and ring bond, fin tube, root block ring, socket and the ring-to-socket junction, rib, saddle socket, saddle ring on the spar, spar wall under the saddle) with CAD dimensions, KP 2.156 for printed and bonded parts and sourced or EST-labelled allowables, including load sharing with the foam groove if credited. Name the true minimum. If any RF < 1 (or < 1.15 if that rule applies, see F6), go down the failure-resolution ladder for that part (for example a longer saddle socket or a printed-orientation rule). Delete or re-label the three baseline-geometry rows in AR-012, the trade table row B and D-213 (3).

### F2 CRITICAL: `landing_a.py` puts the ground 37.5 mm higher than the CAD, so the tip-over PASS (33.0 deg) and the lean scan thresholds are on the wrong geometry
Evidence:
- `landing_a.py` uses `x_foot=0.30`. The CAD ground plane is `X_GROUND = 0.3375` (`electrical.py` l.498; build check "shoe plates ... 337.5 mm aft of the leading edge", `a_build_steps.py` l.487). The film record agrees: pads 0.316 m below the CG (`docs/video/FILM-PROGRESS.md` l.103). The SITL plant reads the CAD value (`sitl/a_plant.py` l.112 `FOOT_X` from `x_ground_m`), so SITL and `landing_a.py` model different aircraft. This may explain part of the "sim vs SITL disagreement".
- Static narrow-axis tip-over with the CAD ground (R5): h = 0.3375 - 0.0229 = 0.3146 m. atan(0.177/0.3146) = **29.4 deg** about the pad centre, 30.2 deg about the pad outer edge (z 0.183). With the CAD CG z offset of 6.4 mm (`FILM-PROGRESS.md` l.103) toward the tipping side: 29.3 deg. **A-P9 requires >= 30 deg** (`REQUIREMENTS-A.md` l.42). D-213 s5 and AR-008 report 33.0 deg PASS.
- Dynamic thresholds, narrow axis, sink 0.34 m/s, mu 0.40, lean in the direction of travel (R6). CAD foot: lateral 0.16 m/s tips at **24 deg** (D-213 reports 28 deg). Lateral 0.40-0.45 m/s tips at 16 deg. Lateral 0.52 m/s tips at **12 deg** (upright at 10 deg). Sim foot (as committed): 0.52 m/s tips at 16 deg and 0.45 m/s at 18 deg, so even on its own geometry D-213 missed this exposure (it scanned lateral 0.16 m/s only). With the lean against the direction of travel, nothing tips up to 18 deg at any lateral speed.
- Rib RF with the CAD foot (R5): minimum still 1.26 (7/7 combo); wide cases drop from 2.40 to 2.06 (bending arm 0.267 m instead of 0.2295 m). Side load and RF are not materially affected; tip-over is.
Required fix: put the CAD ground (`X_GROUND`, pad-centre z) and the CAD CG (`cad_mass.json`, including z) into `landing_a.py` and rerun AR-008 and AR-012. Record A-P9 against the CAD geometry: it is FAIL or zero-margin, so it needs a ladder decision, for example a shorter pad, a wider foot track in z, or a lower CG. This is a requirement decision and must be logged; it must not be moved silently. Add the `X_GROUND` / `x_foot` mismatch to T-066 or open a new task.

### F3 MAJOR: pad friction is a demand-side input with no part behind it, and the decision makes the K09-L control a "fallback"
Evidence: RF = 1 at mu 0.51, 0.90 at 0.60 and 0.79 at 0.70 (R2). A's pad is bare EVA/PE (C9; the BOM has no tape line). K09-L's own record (D-105, `CHANGE-LOG.md` l.764) took bare neoprene at mu 0.7 (38.9 N, 1.59 x its design load) and therefore made the UHMW-PE face a pre-flight item. No landing surface is defined anywhere in A (`MISSION.md` and `REQUIREMENTS-A.md` have no grass, asphalt or mat), so "mu <= 0.40" has no counter-surface, and on grass the foot can snag (effective mu above any Coulomb value). The design then depends on a single unmeasured input with a plausible chance of failing.
Required fix: (a) define the landing surface or surfaces (requirement, flight card). (b) Either adopt the UHMW-PE face now (BOM line with a sourced A price, CAD, step C9; about 0.2 g; D-213's own row F gives RF 1.54) or make the drag test on each defined surface a hold point before the first free landing, with mu_max <= 0.40 measured as static friction (breakaway), not dynamic. (c) State the joint acceptance: side load scales with mu x peak normal (plateau + damping), and the plateau (30 N) is also unmeasured. The acceptance must cover both, for example by rerunning the RF with the measured plateau and mu, not two independent limits.

### F4 MAJOR: the touchdown-envelope cases miss the configuration that the real geometry shows is critical
Evidence: `landing_sitl_env_a.py` applies the lateral speed only in the same sense as the lean (sign +1 mirrored), which is the right worst case, but on the wrong geometry (F2). D-213 says the only exposure is a card-prohibited belly-on landing in 5 m/s. With the CAD foot (R7), every card-allowed SITL record stays upright, but with thin margins: Z5_gb45 (lean 11.0 deg, 0.50 m/s) against a 12 deg threshold at 0.52 m/s, and Lb_5e (12.1 deg, 0.45 m/s) against 16 deg at 0.45 m/s. Lb_5e was edge-on (wind from 90 deg), so applying its lean to the narrow axis is the conservative reading. SITL itself does not tip, but its ground model has artificial angular damping (`a_plant.py`: `al - 8.0*w` when 3 or more feet touch), a viscous lateral friction law capped at 0.6 N, and thrust and control still active. Its "no tip-over" is therefore not physical evidence, and `landing_a.py` (no thrust, no control after 0.6 s) is the pessimistic bound. The truth lies between them and has not been reconciled.
Required fix: reconcile both models on the same geometry. Either add the land-detector and spool-down behaviour to `landing_a.py`, or remove the artificial damping in the SITL plant contact and rerun Z5_b0, Z5_gb45 and Lb_5e. Then set the lean and drift limits from the reconciled boundary.

### F5 MAJOR: the 0.5 m/s drift limit cannot be enforced or observed, and SITL already exceeds it
Evidence: no A.param entry limits horizontal touchdown speed (`A.param` l.116-119, verified by D-213 and consistent with the file). SITL maximum 0.52 m/s (R5_b45) is above the limit. A pilot cannot judge 0.5 m/s by eye at 0.3 m height. The governing structural case (hard 7/7) has no drift at all, so the drift limit does not protect the rib RF. It matters for tip-over (F2, F4).
Required fix: do not use 0.5 m/s as a card limit. Use it as an analysis envelope with margin above the SITL maximum (for example design cases at 0.6-0.7 m/s, labelled EST). Give the card observable items: the landing wind limit by orientation (belly-on <= 3 m/s already), land into the wind, abort or go around if the aircraft is visibly translating or leaning in the direction of travel below about 1 m. Check whether an ArduPilot parameter can bound lean or horizontal speed in the final descent (for example the quadplane angle limit or land-reposition options; parameter names must be verified against the ArduPilot docs before they are cited). Record what the autopilot does and does not enforce.

### F6 MINOR: the acceptance criterion is ambiguous
AR-012 states "RF >= 1.0 ... project 0.15 margin rule would want RF >= 1.15". One criterion must be chosen and cited. The ultimate factor 1.5 x 1.15 is already in KF, so RF >= 1.0 is the usual reading; if the 0.15 rule is project practice, cite where it is set.

### F7 MINOR: the "sourced" 41.3 MPa is a different vendor's product
The 41.3 MPa (Tap Plastics pultruded sheet, test method not stated) is quoted as giving RF 1.67, but the BOM rib is RMRC 14x12 (`BOM-A.csv` R09, layup not stated, "unverified"). Quote 1.67 only as a sensitivity, not as a sourced capacity. The twist coupon must use the RMRC tube actually bought.

### F8 MINOR: reproducibility gaps
- The lean scan (`lean_scan_out.txt`) and the TAU 30 vs 41.3 sensitivity were scratch scripts that were not saved; commit them or fold them into `landing_side_a.py`.
- `landing_sitl_env_a.py` hard-codes an absolute Windows path (`sys.path.insert(0, r"C:\Users\...")`); use the script directory as `landing_side_a.py` does.
- The record says "29 SITL records", but 22 store the field under `landing` and 7 under `land` cases; name the glob.

### F9 MINOR: the proposed claims are not registered
`CL-A-RIB-TWIST-COUPON`, `CL-A-PAD-MU-DRAG` and `CL-A-LANDING-DRIFT-CARD` exist only as text (`T064_landing_side.md` l.102, `analysis-records.md` l.340). They are not in `redesign/qp/A/proofs/claims.*.json`, so the proof graph does not carry the UNVERIFIED status.

### F10 MINOR (observation, outside scope): FIN-005 mass
FIN-005 is listed at 1.0 g (`wing.py` l.533), but a 60 mm PETG ring of OD 21 mm on the 12 mm spar plus a 24 mm socket is roughly 10-18 g solid (EST, before infill and the clip slot). The mass entry should be checked against the CAD volume.

## 3. Answers to the five questions

1. **Is withdrawing the 14 N cap legitimate? Yes.** D-105 (`CHANGE-LOG.md` l.764) and D-138 (l.824) set K09-L's 24.5 N equal to its own sim result (24.28 N, `CL-C5-SIM3D-SIDE-12`, CLOSURE C5.md l.191), and the structure was then designed to it (shoe FEA 24.96 N, l.277). C5.md l.141-142: "set by the plateau and mu, not by mass". The cap was a demand, and scaling it by mass had no basis. Replacing it with capacity is the correct move and is not goalpost-moving in itself. What K09-L actually carried as a control was **mu <= 0.40 enforced by a pad face**. A dropped that face, so it must be restored or measured (F3).
2. **Is the rib-root check right? Arithmetic yes, scope no.** The rib section, factors, interaction and time step are correct (R1-R4, hand check RF 1.256). But the load path in the record (foot -> rib -> fin) is not A's. A's path is pad -> PETG shoe -> fin tube -> PETG root block -> rib -> PETG saddle -> spar. The printed end joints and the pad attachment are unchecked and plausibly weaker (F1), and the foot point sits 37.5 mm off the CAD (F2). The 30 MPa shear EST is plausible for an unknown-layup tube but unsourced for the RMRC part (F7).
3. **Is "PASS, UNVERIFIED until coupon + drag test" the right status?** Not yet. "PASS (calc)" requires the calculation to cover the limiting part, and it does not (F1). After F1 and F2 are fixed, "PASS (calc, EST allowables), UNVERIFIED" would be right. The three claims are necessary but not sufficient. Also needed: (a) a claim for each printed joint (saddle and root block, coupon or calculation); (b) a claim for the pad attachment (PSA shear and peel at 1.2 m/s with side load, which can be the same drop rig as the plateau coupon); (c) a landing-surface definition attached to `CL-A-PAD-MU-DRAG`, with mu measured static; (d) a plateau measurement folded into the side-load acceptance; (e) registration in `proofs/claims.*.json` (F9).
4. **Is dynamic tip-over a real risk for normal landings, and what card limit?** On the CAD geometry it is real, not only for prohibited belly-on 5 m/s landings. Static narrow-axis tip-over is about 29.4 deg, below A-P9's 30 deg (F2). When the lean is in the direction of travel, the pessimistic sim tips at 24 deg (0.16 m/s), 16 deg (0.40-0.45 m/s) and 12 deg (0.52 m/s). Card-allowed SITL touchdowns reach 11.0-12.1 deg lean at 0.45-0.50 m/s, within 1-4 deg of those thresholds. Interim card limit, sourced to R6/R7 and labelled EST until F4 reconciliation: **touchdown lean <= 10 deg when any visible translation, and abort or go around above 10 deg or when translating in the direction of the lean below about 1 m**; land into the wind; belly-on only up to 3 m/s (kept). The 10 deg comes from the R6 scan: upright at 10 deg at 0.52 m/s, tips at 12 deg. Lean is hard for a pilot to judge, so the lasting fix is in the design or autopilot: restore tip-over margin (F2 ladder) and bound lean in the final descent.
5. **Is a 0.5 m/s drift limit on 29 SITL records, with no enforcing parameter, acceptable?** Not as a card limit (F5). It cannot be observed, SITL exceeds it (0.52), the structural governing case does not depend on it, and the tip-over risk at that speed is the larger issue. It is acceptable as an analysis envelope only with margin above the SITL maximum, and alongside observable card items and a check of what ArduPilot can enforce.
