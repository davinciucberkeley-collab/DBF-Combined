## Elastic checks

A flexing feature may pass into the body it rides over only by the deflection its strain limit allows. Cantilever finger of free length L and thickness t, tip deflection d: root strain eps = 3 t d / (2 L^2) (Roark, end-loaded cantilever; the snap-fit relation of the Bayer / Covestro snap-fit guide). A board pressed end-on over its length L: eps = d / L. d is the deepest interference the sweep measured between exactly these two bodies (assembly_plan.ELASTIC), never more than the row cap. EST: stand-in geometry, print-dependent allowable.

## Why the brace is not bowed in

BUILD-GUIDE 4.5 said the brace `FIN-009` is "bowed into its slots, anchor end first, then sprung into the collar ear". Both ends sit 10 mm deep in blind slots cut to the strip section. With the anchor end seated, the strip must bow until its chord is 10.5 mm shorter to clear the ear. Clamped at both ends (first buckling mode), free length 119.6 mm of 139.6 mm: bow amplitude a = (2/pi) sqrt(L d) = 22.6 mm, peak strain eps = pi^2 a t / L^2 = **3.12 %** against 1.30 % ultimate for pultruded carbon (brace ends 10 mm in each slot: interfaces E-FIN-37..44; CF strip 10 x 2 (parts FIN-009); pultruded carbon ultimate tensile strain 1.30 % (TAP Plastics pultruded carbon rods & tubes sheet, https://www.tapplastics.com/image/pdf/Pultruded_Carbon_Rods__Tubes.pdf, checked 2026-11-13); EST). It would break the strip, so the check does not allow it. Instead the brace goes into the anchor and the collar ear on the bench, each slid along the brace axis, and the three go on as one rigid sub-assembly: the collar down the motor tube while the anchor drops into its pocket on the spar (step 4.5a, swept).

## What this check does not prove

- The final pose of every part: that is the collision gate (`collision/collide.py`, `--glb`). This check starts 0.05 mm off the seat.
- Tape, straps and wires are flexible: they are tested where they lie, not as a rigid sweep; a wire is also failed if it is laid after a tape it lies against.
- A body is never tested against itself (as in the gate): two pieces of one CAD body, such as a lead split at its connector or the two halves of a hinge-tape strip at its fold, are one body; so are the strands of one wire.
- Hands, tools and clearance for them (a hex key on a pinch bolt, a soldering iron): not modelled.
- Gravity: no dynamics; a part counts as supported when it touches something placed or a named fixture.
- Bench sub-assemblies are checked against their bench-mates only, then moved on as one body.
- Real Fusion geometry (UNVERIFIED-IN-FUSION, as the gate).
