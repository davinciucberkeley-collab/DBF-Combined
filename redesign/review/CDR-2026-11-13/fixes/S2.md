## 2. The tooling fix (CDR-03)

**What was wrong.** `proofkit.margin()` returned `None` when the limit was 0 (it divides by the limit), and
`build_graph.py` marked a claim as failing only when the margin was a negative number. So a claim such as
"margin of safety −0.34 against ≥ 0" had no margin, no `fails` flag, and was missing from the fail count, the
requirement roll-up, `--strict` and the viewer's "Fails only" filter.

**What changed.**

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `proofs/proofkit.py` | `margin()`: for a limit of 0 the margin is the signed distance to the limit in the claim's unit (`value` for `>= 0`, `−value` for `<= 0`); other limits unchanged (fraction of the limit). NaN gives `None`. New `margin_text()` prints a percentage, or for a limit of 0 the distance (`-0.00012 m abs`). `Kit.write()` uses it |
| `proofs/build_graph.py` | `_st_claim` sets `margin_abs: true` on limit-0 claims and fails on the sign. New `self_check()`: every claim with a numeric value and limit is judged a second way (plain comparison); a missing margin or a `fails` flag that disagrees is a build error `SELF-CHECK …`. A limit with a non-numeric value gives a warning. `CHAIN.md` prints limit-0 margins as distances and counts them separately in the "Smallest margin" column |
| `proofs/viewer/app.js`, `model.js` | limit-0 margins shown as a distance with the unit, not a percentage; they are kept out of the "worst margin" percentage of a requirement |
| `proofs/test_proofs.py` | 3 new tests (8 in all, pass): `margin()` on both sides of 0 and at 0; eight limit-0 claims through a real build (fail flags, count, `--strict`); `self_check()` catches the old behaviour |
| `proofs/SCHEMA.md`, `proofs/README.md` | the rule and the self-check written down |

Checks run: `python -I redesign/proofs/test_proofs.py` 8 tests OK; `node redesign/proofs/viewer/check_viewer.js` OK;
`python -I redesign/proofs/build_graph.py --examples` OK (2 fails, as before). The full build reports no
`SELF-CHECK` error.
