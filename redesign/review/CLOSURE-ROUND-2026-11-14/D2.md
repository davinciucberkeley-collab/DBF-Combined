### D-170.1 Why the bay's free length cannot do it alone

- The most forward pack centroid is set by the crush stack, not by the bay length:
  - x_fwd = x_fs + spreader 1.5 + block 31.0 (A1's D-150 floor) + L/2.
  - With the 139 mm pack and today's stop face −0.1382, that is **−0.0362**.
- The bay's free length (185.7 − 139 = 46.7 mm) is 32.5 mm of block + spreader ahead of the pack and 14.2 mm of
  spacers behind it. All of the free room that can move the pack is already behind it.
- Shortening the bay at the aft end (aft stop forward) would only replace spacers with longer strut ribs. That is about
  the same mass (+2.9 g for 18 mm of three 3 × 14 mm ribs against +2.7 g of spacers) and C3 would have to re-check the
  strut. I kept the aft stop at 0.0475.
- So the stop face has to move forward: x_fs ≤ −0.0547 − 0.0695 − 0.0015 − 0.031 = −0.1567. The nose wall + bond line
  + stop stack is 8.8 mm and is already at its minimum (`CL-D2-NOSE-TO-STOP` ≤ 8.85 mm). That gives x_le ≤ −0.1655:
  18.5 mm.
- **The 2 mm spacer grid sets the last step.** Stations are x_as − L/2 − stack = −0.022 − (even mm).
  - −0.054 is too far aft.
  - −0.056 needs a 34 mm stack and x_fwd ≤ −0.056, so x_le = −0.167: **20 mm**.
  - A 19 mm nose would need the aft stop moved 1 mm as well, which changes the strut, so I did not take it.
- C5's per-mass block for 479 g is 27.5 × 479 / 551 = 24.0 mm `EST` (`CL-C5-BLOCK-L` scaled). If C5 and A1 re-derive
  the 31.0 mm floor for the lighter pack, the nose could be about 7 mm shorter. I did **not** take that: it is a crash
  limit owned by C5 / A1.
