## 1. `CL-B3-HOVER-YAW-WTIP-20` (R-HOVER-AUTHORITY, R-WIND)

### 1.1 Cause
With a wingtip into a 5 m/s wind (`Q_WVANE_LAND` / `TAKEOFF` 3) and a 20° heading error, the wing acts as a low-aspect-ratio
plate. Its yaw moment is 0.417 N·m and grows with the heading error. The yaw control at 30 % differential is
4 dT (kq_iso f_low + r sin cant) = 0.550 N·m (`CALC-B3-HOVER-WTIP`, `team/B3/sc_analysis.py` l.853–899).

### 1.4 Recommendation: 9.0° (decides D-127, which proposed 10°)
- **Why 9°, not 10°.** The wing-moment model is `UNVERIFIED` (`ASM-B3-WTIP-CN` CDc 2.0, `ASM-B3-WTIP-ARM`). It is already
  16 % above the reviewer's own estimate (0.42 vs 0.36 N·m at 20°, CDR-AE-04). At 9° the ratio holds 1.5 with the
  cross-flow CDc 10 % higher (2.2). 10° buys 8 % more ratio for 2 mm more stack and 0.3 % more thrust loss, and the extra
  stack lands on a structure (C4) that already fails a frequency claim. The twin test `T-B3-HOVER-YAW-WTIP` stays the
  decider. If it measures a wing moment above 1.045 × this model, the contingency is 10° with `face_ahead` 14.0 mm (gap
  26.1 mm, E1 function).
- **Exact geometry for D3 (unchanged sense, D-52 / SPEC.md "Cant sense at 7°", `cant_sense_UR` −1).** Every motor's
  thrust side component is tangential, magnitude T sin 9°. In (y, z) cruise axes, (y, z)/r = (0.32, 0.19)/0.372 =
  (0.860, 0.511):

  | Station | Position (y, z) m | Side-component direction (y, z) | In words (prop end leans…) | Yaw moment sense |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | UR | (+0.32, +0.19) | (+0.511, −0.860) | outboard (right) and toward the wing plane (down) | same as UR/LL reaction torque (CW group) |
  | UL | (−0.32, +0.19) | (−0.511, −0.860) | outboard (left) and toward the wing plane | CCW group |
  | LR | (+0.32, −0.19) | (+0.511, +0.860) | outboard (right) and toward the wing plane (up) | CCW group |
  | LL | (−0.32, −0.19) | (−0.511, +0.860) | outboard (left) and toward the wing plane | CW group |

  Check: M_x = y F_z − z F_y gives −0.372 F sin c for UR and LL and +0.372 F sin c for UL and LR, with arm r = 0.372 m.
  This matches the diagonal spin pairs in `SPEC.md` (UR + LL CW, UL + LR CCW). Net side force is 0. Wedge 9.0°; print
  groups unchanged (UR + LL print A, UL + LR print B, `propulsion.stations.cant_deg` note).
- **`face_ahead` 7.5 → 12.0 mm.** 11.0 mm is the least that gives 25 mm (25.29 mm). 12.0 mm gives 26.29 mm and keeps E1's
  +1 mm practice of D-61. The hub moves about 4.5 mm forward along the axis, so `x_prop_plane_m` goes from about −0.0127
  to about −0.017 (`EST`; D3's `CL-D3-X-PROP` gives the exact value).

## 2. `CL-B3-TRANS-PITCH-USE` (R-TRANSITION)

### 2.1 Quasi-steady re-check: one model error found
`CALC-B3-TRANSITION` adds B4's prop H-force moment to |M_aero| "with no sign credit". The sign is known. B4 says the
force "acts at the prop hubs … add it as a prop normal-force term" (`team/B4/CHANGE-REQUESTS.md` CR-B4-26). The props sit
ahead of the CG (x −0.0127 vs 0.039–0.051 m), so it is **nose-up**, the same sense as the Ribner term already in M_aero
(`flight_models.py` l.174–175, "props ahead: nose-up"). The wing moment it acts against is **nose-down** (M_aero
−1.27 N·m). Adding its magnitude counts the same physics twice and with the wrong sign. Corrected values at the forward
CG, level flight:

| Prop term | max use @30 % | Note |
|---|---|---|
| carried (H magnitude added) | 0.909 | the claim as written |
| Ribner `ASM-B3-PROP-CNA` 0.11 | 0.830 | = `-NOHUB-CAP30` |
| **B4 BEMT slope (dH/di 0.633 vs Ribner 1.005 N/rad at 15.6 m/s): smaller nose-up term = conservative** | **0.841** | proposed basis |
| B4 H at hover thrust, signed | 0.782 | optimistic bound |

This is a model correction with a stated reason. It does not close the claim.

### 2.2 Time-domain model of the pitch-over (`round1114.py` `simulate`)
**What the firmware does** (read from `ArduPlane/tailsitter.cpp`, tag `Plane-4.7.2`,
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArduPilot/ardupilot/Plane-4.7.2/ArduPlane/tailsitter.cpp, checked 2026-11-14,
state `ANGLE_WAIT_FW`):
- `nav_pitch_cd` ramps down at `transition_rate_fw` (`Q_TAILSIT_RAT_FW` 12°/s).
- Throttle: `set_throttle_out(MAX(motors->get_throttle_hover(), attitude_control->get_throttle_in()), true, 0)`, with the
  source comment "set throttle at either hover throttle or current throttle, whichever is higher, through the
  transition". The pilot throttle stick is not read in this state. Because `set_throttle_out` stores its argument as the
  new throttle-in, the value is **frozen at mode entry**.
- The copter attitude controller (motors) keeps pitch until |pitch| > `Q_TAILSIT_ANGLE` 70°. After that the plane
  controller flies, and the motors still stabilise when `assisted_flight` is set (`Q_ASSIST_SPEED` 14).

**Model.** 2-D rigid body. Wing and prop forces and the pitch moment come from B3's own functions (`FM.wing_coeffs` and the
CP law of `transition_point`). Inputs are those of `CALC-B3-TRANSITION`. Iyy = 0.0527 kg m² (A3, scaled to 2.6 kg).
Thrust is T0 (1 − V_axial / 23.3 m/s). The lapse is fitted to B4's fixed-rpm `CL-B4-SIM-T-I45` 0.527; at fixed throttle
the prop unloads less, so this is conservative. The attitude follows the ramp through a 2nd-order loop, ωn 6 rad/s,
swept 4–10. The motor moment needed is Iyy θ̈ − M_aero (inverse dynamics). Pitch damping is omitted, which is
conservative: it is nose-up while the nose pitches down. After 70° the model flies the level attitude capped 2° below the
stall, with FW throttle 0.70 / 0.49 × hover (`EST`) and attached elevons first.

**Results** (`td_summary`; "trim" = max |M_aero| / 1.541 N·m over the pitch-over; "po" = max motor moment / cap):

What the model shows:
1. **At 12°/s the time-domain trim peak (0.84) equals the corrected quasi-steady value.** The firmware holds hover
   throttle while the thrust lapses (T/W ≈ 0.57 at the peak). The aircraft therefore follows nearly the level path, so the
   quasi-steady number is not over-conservative. The inertial term is ≤ 0.10 N·m (0.07 of the cap), and only at the
   handover.
2. **At the peak the lower pair has only about 1.9 N left after the pitch differential.** The 30 %-of-hover differential
   (2.03 N) is more than half of what each motor makes there. Raising the transition differential to 40 % would take the
   lower pair to about 0.8 N with no roll or yaw room. That is the same objection as CDR-AE-07.
3. **A faster pitch rate or an earlier handover only moves the problem.** The pitch-over peak drops (0.55 at 30°/s), but
   the handover then happens at 9.5–11 m/s, below the tripped stall. In the assisted FW phase the wing is stalled for
   0.45–0.6 s and needs 0.83–0.87 of the cap (`peak_fw_assist`). My FW-phase model is crude, but the cause (handover
   below Vs) is robust.
4. **Thrust is the lever.** More entry thrust makes the aircraft climb and accelerate at a lower angle of attack, so it
   reaches 70° at 14.1 m/s (≥ `Q_ASSIST_SPEED` 14, above Vs) with attached flow. Trim peak: 0.35 at the forward CG.
   1.3 W only just passes (0.59). **1.4 W static is the recommended floor.**

### 2.3 What the 0.60 limit is and where it applies
- **Source.** `ASM-B3-TRANS-USE-MAX` (`team/B3/sc_analysis.py` l.115–117): "Fraction of the motors' pitch differential the
  quasi-steady transition may use", basis "keeps 40 % for pitch rate, gusts and roll/yaw mixing; B3 choice". It was set
  against the full differential. CDR-16 re-based the denominator to the 30 % cap and kept 0.60.
- **Check of the value.** At the quasi-steady worst point (forward CG, 11 m/s, α 37.5°):
  - a 2 m/s vertical gust changes the moment by 0.544 N·m (0.353 of the cap);
  - the `R-HOVER-AUTHORITY` floor of 2.0 rad/s² on Iyy is 0.105 N·m (0.068);
  - the reserve is therefore 0.42, which leaves 0.58 for trim;
  - with a 3 m/s gust the reserve is 0.60.

  **So 0.60 is right in value** (≈ a 2 m/s gust plus the rate floor, inside the pitch budget alone). The "roll/yaw mixing"
  words are wrong now, because the per-axis 30 % cap already holds that share. Restated basis: "reserve on the pitch axis
  for a 2 m/s vertical gust at the worst transition point plus the 2.0 rad/s² rate floor (round1114.json
  `reserve_at_qs_worst`)". Value unchanged.
- **Peak or sustained?** It applies to the **trim** part, i.e. the maximum of |M_aero| along the flown trajectory. The trim
  persists for seconds (0.5 s moving minimum 0.75 vs peak 0.84 at 12°/s), so in practice it is the peak of the trim. It
  does not apply to the Iyy θ̈ transient, which belongs to the rate reserve. The 3 m/s gust column
  (`peak_with_gust3`) is information, not a pass line. Near the stall boundary the gust model overstates, because a
  restoring up-gust moment is counted as a demand.

### 2.5 Decision D-128 (proposed by S3, delegated to us)
**Accepted for the 8 m/s hover gust only (`ASM-B3-GUST-DIFF-MAX` 0.40). Not extended to transition.**
- Hover in the 8 m/s gust: B1 `thrust_frac_8ms` 0.618 × 13.5 N = 8.37 N mean (`CALC-B1-WIND`). With the 1.2125 split, the
  upper pair is about 9.17 N and the lower pair 7.57 N.
- 40 % (2.70 N) gives upper 11.87 N ≤ 13.5 N and lower 4.87 N > 0. The pitch ratio becomes 1.25 (`CL-B3-HOVER-PITCH-RATIO-GUST`).
- If yaw also asks for its full 30 % (2.03 N) at the same instant, the upper motor reaches 13.9 N, 3 % over. ArduPilot's
  mixer gives pitch and roll priority over yaw (yaw headroom is cut first), so this is acceptable for a survive-and-land
  case.
- In transition, §2.2 item 2 rules 40 % out.
