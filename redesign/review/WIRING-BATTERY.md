# K09-L build review: wiring, power distribution and battery

Reviewer: electrical integration ("how would we actually build this?"), 2026-11-11, branch `redesign/v1`.
Scope: pack bay and retention, AS150U, power stack, bus board, UBEC and fuse, ESC leads and caps, the wing and fin
grooves, phase splices, FC, GPS and compass, RX, airspeed and pitot line. Sources: `subsystems/{harness,power,avionics,pod}/`,
`team/F1..F4, E2, E3, D2`, `params/params.json`, `params/CHANGE-LOG.md`, the three showcase renders and `hover-pose.png`.
I did not make new Blender renders: the showcase renders and the CAD coordinates in the SPECs were enough for these
findings. Every number below is quoted from the repo or a cited web source, or it is labelled as my estimate (`REVIEWER EST`).

## Verdict

The electrical design is careful on paper and mostly right on gauges: 10 AWG mains, 16 AWG ESC leads at an 8.4 A
hover share, an AS150U rated well above the 30.3 A continuous need, a cap at every ESC, and no solder joints buried in
the foam grooves (every splice sits on a fin face or in the pod). Building it is the weak part. Four things stop me from
handing it to a student crew as written:

- **The CoreWing stack split is unproven.** The pod's power layout assumes the CoreWing power board separates from the
  FC and talks to it over a 6-pin GH link without Vx, with outputs on FC pads. None of that is published, and the
  cited sources disagree even on the servo BEC rating.
- **The two BUILD files contradict each other** on when the ESC leads are soldered to the bus bars.
- **The wing-fin harness has no disconnect.** One lead fault or one broken fin means peeling the skin tape and
  desoldering 10 AWG and 8 AWG joints inside a foam-board pod.
- **The only safety disconnect sits inside the hatch.** It is a stiff connector in a cradle hung on nylon screws, and
  it is also the only post-crash kill on an unfused pack.

Buy one CoreWing V2, one ESC 4-pack and one pack now and measure them. Add a connector per ESC pair inside the pod.
Put the AS150U on a short flying pigtail. Then the pod CAD can be frozen for FEA. Wire gauge, voltage drop and compass
interference do not need more analysis before CFD/FEA. They need bench tests that are already planned.

## Build steps as I'd actually do them

Order assumes WB-01 is closed (board measured), WB-03 is adopted (one plug per ESC pair in the pod) and WB-04 is
adopted (AS150U pigtail).

1. **Arrival bench (week 1).** Measure the CoreWing V2 boards, one ESC, one pack, the UBEC (if kept), the AS150U, the
   caps, and the OD of every wire reel. Flash the FC with Plane 4.7 and the ESC with Bluejay. Bench-test bdshot over a
   0.7 m 28 AWG twisted lead. Write the numbers into the params files, then freeze the pod CAD.
2. **Bench: ESC sub-assemblies (×4 + 1 spare).** Solder a 16 AWG pair, cut to length + 30 mm, to each ESC's pads from
   below. Twist it. Solder the cap legs to the same pads, bed the can in silicone, and fit adhesive heat-shrink. Splice
   the 28 AWG DShot pair to the factory signal lead. Crimp the plug half on the pod end **after** threading (step 5).
   Continuity, polarity, then a 10 N pull test.
3. **Bench: power stack, out of the pod.** Make the bus board: FR4 with two tinned 8 AWG bars, then four short 16 AWG
   pigtails with the aircraft-side plugs, plus the 20 AWG fuse feed. Mount the power board on the deck with the
   supplied 470 µF 35 V cap at its battery pads. Fit the 10 AWG pigtail with the AS150U aircraft half. Fit the
   `W-MAIN-2` jumpers to the bars. Do all of this with an 80–100 W iron in free air. Check the loop resistance
   (`T-F1-DROP` coupon) and > 1 MΩ between rails.
4. **Wing (before skinning).** Rout the grooves to the measured OD + 0.5 mm with a depth sled. Drill P1 and the fin
   holes, then glue the straws and grommets. Dry-fit an offcut of each wire for the full length.
5. **Fins.** Strap each ESC on its pad. Thread the free end of the pair inboard through the grommet, leaving a
   20–30 mm service loop on the fin face. Tie at the boss lug. Lay the wire in the grooves (the lower pairs via P1) and
   tape-tab it. Bring the end into the pod through the side exit. Crimp or solder the plug half on now.
6. **Phase leads.** Measure the motor lead reach first. If an extension is needed, make staggered splices with an
   outer sleeve and the two ties. Label the phase order. Hand-spin the motor and check for ≥ 5 mm clearance off the
   bell.
7. **Skin the wing** over the grooves. Run a finger along each run and check that nothing stands proud.
8. **Pod.** Drop the pre-wired stack onto the inserts (4 screws, no soldering in the pod). Mate the four ESC plugs at
   the exits and tie them in their lanes. Fit the UBEC if kept, then the FC on gel with its pigtails to the JR bar. Run
   the GH cables: GPS up the leg, then RX, airspeed and the power-board link, with the current-sense wire twisted.
9. **Props off. Power-up check.** Full continuity and isolation check with the pack out. Then plug the pack through an
   inline smoke stopper or current-limited supply first. Check the 6 V rail, 5 V, I²C scan, ESC beeps and motor
   direction from `MOTOR_TEST`.
10. **Stand tests.** `T-F1-RIPPLE` at each ESC, `T-F1-DROP`, current-sense calibration with the motors running,
    compassmot, `T-F1-WIRE-HEAT` on the straw and groove coupon.
11. **Field kit per pack.** Label each pack and tether its spacer stack and block to it. Weigh the pack, then do the
    CG balance check.
12. **Field card.** TX on → aircraft flat on the cradle → pack in → plug → hatch open until pre-arm passes → pitot
    lines → hatch → stand it up → arm by switch. Crash: e-stop, approach from behind, open the hatch, pull the plug,
    bag the pack.
