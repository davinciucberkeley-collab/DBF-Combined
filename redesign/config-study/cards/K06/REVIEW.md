# K06 skeptic review (2026-11-09)

Reviewer role: try to refute the K06 card numbers. I did not edit the card. I re-ran a copy of `sizing.py` in a scratch folder (`python -I`) and it reproduces `sizing.json` exactly (MTOW 2,389 g, hover 380 W, T/W 1.79 with sag, cruise 17.1 min, box 0.88 × 0.50 × 0.39 m). The arithmetic is right. My challenges are about the inputs. Corrected runs patch the inputs listed in C1–C4 and use the card's own equations.

Tags: `ESTIMATE` = my engineering guess, `UNVERIFIED` = not checked against a primary source.

## Verdict

The concept holds up. It is a scaled-up E-flite Convergence or Flightory Stallion layout, and ArduPilot has documented support for it. The card is **optimistic on mass (about +0.25 kg), cruise drag and stall speed**, and it misses a few small costs. **T/W drops from 1.79 to about 1.62 with sag, which is right at the 1.6 gate.** That is the one result that matters, and the card's "0.28 kg headroom" is mostly used up by the corrections below.

## Challenges

| # | Claim on card | Challenge and evidence | Corrected estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Printed PETG pod + hatch weighs 160 g | The card's own build plan gives "about 30 h of unattended printing" for the pod. Thin-wall PETG at a 0.4 mm nozzle lays down roughly 8–15 g/h (`ESTIMATE`), so 30 h is about 250–400 g. A shell check gives the same answer: 0.46 m × 0.37 m perimeter × 0.8 mm × 1.27 g/cm³ ≈ 175 g for the bare skin, before the wing saddle, joiner sockets, hatch, bulkheads and boom clamp. | **280 g** `ESTIMATE` (+120 g) |
| C2 | Each tilt nacelle (PETG, 6 mm steel pivot, 2 bearings) weighs 45 g | The 6 mm steel shaft (~60 mm long) is 13 g and two F686ZZ bearings are about 7 g. That leaves 25 g of PETG to hold a 63 g standard-size servo, a 3108 motor mount and about 15 N of thrust, and to survive a tip-over. | **65 g each** `ESTIMATE` (+40 g total) |
| C3 | Packing-tape skin is 60 g/m² | Scotch Heavy Duty tape is a 3.1 mil (79 µm) film plus adhesive. The card itself says "weigh a sample" (budget-airframe M2). | 75 g/m² `ESTIMATE` (+13 g) |
| C4 | Mass list is complete | Items missing from the list: battery tray, strap and Velcro (15 g); servo extensions and the 6-pin plugs to the nacelles and elevons, which run ≈ 0.5 m each (25 g); GPS mast, ESC capacitor and heat-shrink (10 g). The 10 % contingency was meant for glue and paint, not for these. | +50 g `ESTIMATE` |
| C5 | MTOW 2.39 kg | Sum of C1–C4: non-battery + contingency goes from 1.69 to 1.94 kg, so **MTOW is 2.63 kg**. Check against a comparable aircraft: the Flightory Stallion VTOL is a printed tilt-tri with 2 front tilt motors and 1 fixed rear motor, 1,340 mm span, and **AUW 2.0–3.0 kg** ([air-rc](https://www.air-rc.com/aircraft/Flightory_Stallion-VTOL_stallion), [flightory.com](https://flightory.com/?p=3755), retailer data). It uses 7 in props and micro tilt servos (manual text extracted 2026-11-09). K06 at 2.4–2.6 kg is in the right range. The smaller Convergence is 650 mm and 771 g ([skyraccoon](https://www.skyraccoon.com/aircraft/E-flite_Convergence_EFL11075)). | **2.63 kg** (0.25 kg growth allowance kept) |
| C6 | Hover 380 W, T/W 1.79 with sag, ~0.28 kg headroom | The X3108S table (1,580 gf at 30 A, 750 g at 104 W, 7.2 g/W) traces to the SunnySky data image cited in `docs/research/budget-lift.md` B1. I could not re-fetch it (HTTP 429) and found no independent test, so it stays `UNVERIFIED`. The table was measured with an APC 11x4.7; the card flies an 11x4.5MR, which is a small difference. Momentum theory with FM·η ≈ 0.5 gives about 160 W/kg, which is plausible for 11 in props on 4S. The physics checks out; the mass does not. At 2.63 kg the result is **451 W, 30.5 A, T/W 1.80 on the table and 1.62 with sag**. The 0.9 sag factor matches about 3.5 V/cell. At 3.4 V/cell late in a flight, (13.6/14.8)² ≈ 0.85 gives **T/W ≈ 1.53, below the gate**. | **Hover ≈ 450 W, 30 A; T/W 1.62 (fresh) to 1.53 (end of pack)** |
| C7 | Equal thrust share because x_r = 2·x_f | The hover thrust split is set by the CG, and a tailless wing's CG is fixed by aerodynamics. CG at 167 mm is about 22 % MAC (MAC LE ≈ 0.109 m from root LE). With the wing a.c. near 25 % MAC, the static margin is about 3 % before counting the destabilising tractor nacelles 0.33 m ahead and the pod. That is marginal. Moving the CG forward for a safer margin changes the motor shares. The opposite mistake also hurts: if the CG ends up 3 cm aft of plan, the rear motor carries 38 % instead of 33 %, which leaves the rear motor about 1.5:1 thrust headroom and makes it the first to saturate in pitch. | Set the CG for the static margin first, then place the rear motor. Rear-motor T/W margin is about **1.4–1.6**, not 1.79 `ESTIMATE` |
| C8 | CLmax 0.85, Vs 11.5 m/s, transition at 15 m/s | 0.85 is generous for a reflexed tailless section trimmed with up-elevon; 0.70–0.80 is more typical `ESTIMATE`. Wing loading also goes to 7.8 kg/m², which is high for a 1.3 m foam flying wing. With CLmax 0.75 at 2.63 kg: **Vs 12.9 m/s and 1.3 Vs = 16.8 m/s.** Transition takes longer and needs more speed. | Vs ≈ 13 m/s, transition ≥ 17 m/s |
| C9 | Cruise L/D 8.2, 108 W, CD0 0.040, η_prop 0.55 | CD0 0.040 has to cover a pod, two nacelles with standard servos, a boom, three skids, tip fins and a stopped rear prop. 0.045–0.055 is more realistic `ESTIMATE`. A J = 0.4 operating point on a 4.5 in pitch MR prop is close to its zero-thrust advance ratio (pitch speed at 8,300 rpm ≈ 15.8 m/s), where efficiency falls off, so 0.50 is safer. At CD0 0.050, η_prop 0.50, CLmax 0.75 and 2.63 kg: **cruise is 17 m/s, L/D 6.7, 176 W, needing 9,100 rpm of about 11,300 available (sagging toward ~10,400).** | **Cruise ≈ 175 W; L/D ≈ 6.7** |
| C10 | Endurance: 2 min hover + 17 min cruise on 4S 5000 | Corrected run: hover-only 6.3 min, **cruise 9.4 min after 2 min hover** (20 % reserve). The mission is now a **VTOL demonstrator with a short cruise** (team lead, 2026-11-09), so the card's M-A 10-min objective no longer applies. 9 min is plenty. The 4S 3300 pack gives 4.9 min of cruise after the corrections, which is enough for a demo circuit and saves about 120 g. Keep the 5000 packs for margin. | 9.4 min cruise; 5000 pack OK |
| C11 | Peak currents 34 A in transition, AS150U | Corrected values: transition about 38–40 A, all motors at full throttle about 90 A. The AS150U is still fine. Each half-wing XT60 carries about 10 A in hover, which is fine. | OK |
| C12 | Servo power | Checked and refuted my own concern: the CoreWing F405 Wing V2 servo BEC is rated **8 A continuous / 14 A peak at 5, 6 or 7.2 V**. It has 12 outputs (K06 needs 7), current sensing to 90 A, and an ArduPilot target ([ArduPilot docs](https://ardupilot.org/plane/docs/common-CoreWingF405WingV2.html)). The docs say the board "does not include all ArduPilot features" because of flash limits. Confirm QuadPlane tilt-rotor is in the build before ordering `UNVERIFIED`. The Kakute H743-Wing fallback removes that doubt for +$40. | OK, with one firmware check |
| C13 | Cost $1,649, reserve $351 | Missing items. **(a)** The RadioMaster Pocket/Pocket Crush needs **2 × 18650 cells, not included** ([getfpv](https://www.getfpv.com/radios/radio-controllers/radiomaster-pocket-radio-cc2500-elrs-2-4ghz.html), [rotorvillage](https://rotorvillage.ca/radiomaster-pocket-crush-transmitter-elrs/)), about $15. **(b)** FAA registration is $5. **(c)** A LiPo voltage checker/alarm is about $8. **(d)** Spare props: 9 props for 3 positions is thin for 3–4 weeks of hover tuning; add 6 for about $23. Tax on (a)–(d): about $5. Epoxy is already in the hardware kit (budget-airframe §3, line 13). Jacobs Hall Maker Pass fees could apply if the course does not cover them `UNVERIFIED` (search budget ran out before I could check). Overseas items (ESCs, CF tubes) carry tariff/price-swing risk. | **Total ≈ $1,705; reserve ≈ $295.** One bad crash ($340) would still use up the reserve. |
| C14 | Packed box 0.88 × 0.50 × 0.39 m, 8 min assembly, 1 person | Plausible. The 0.12 m stack height per half needs the nacelle parked in the cruise position (motor forward) when packed, and the width sits exactly at the 0.50 m limit. Either way a 0.9 × 0.5 × 0.4 m box fits any sedan trunk, so the limit is self-imposed. | OK `ESTIMATE` |
| C15 | ~120 person-hours to build | Light for a first scratch-built tilt-rotor. Not counted: hot-wire template iteration, CG ballasting and re-trim, compass/ESC/servo-direction setup (the ArduPilot tilt-rotor threads report reversed tilt servos and confusing ESC wiring), vibration isolation, and rebuilds after the first crash. The plan has 4–5 weeks of build plus 3–4 weeks of tuning. That fits 12 weeks only if nothing slips. | **≈ 180 person-hours** `ESTIMATE`; still fits 12 weeks with 3–4 students |
| C16 | Controls lineage `UNVERIFIED` | Mostly confirmed. ArduPilot's tilt-rotor guide documents vectored yaw (`Q_TILT_TYPE 2`, plus `Q_TILT_YAW_ANGLE`, which the card omits; it should be about 10°) ([ArduPilot](https://ardupilot.org/plane/docs/guide-tilt-rotor.html)). The Convergence and Stallion both use 2 front tilt motors, 1 fixed rear motor and yaw from front tilt. Community AR-Pro and Dolphin tilt-tri conversions fly this way ([forum](https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/ar-pro-wing-conversion-to-tilttri-quadplane/79697)). This is the best-documented small-VTOL layout after the plain quadplane. The remaining risk is a single point of failure in hover (any motor, ESC or tilt servo) plus tailless pitch trim through transition. The rear-torque yaw trim is small: the front pair needs about 1.5° of differential tilt for ≈ 0.12 N·m (calc: rear motor ~90 W mech at ~730 rad/s; 2 × 7.85 N × 0.30 m × sin δ). | Controls risk **3/5** |
| C17 | 329TG tilt servos (63 g, 1.9 N·m) | They are oversized for a pivot on the thrust line: the gyroscopic load is 0.17 N·m. The Stallion uses micro servos with 7 in props. A ~25–40 g metal-gear servo would save about 50–70 g at no extra risk. Optional. | Possible −60 g |
