# D9. Spare X4110S motor

**Recommendation:** Do not buy the spare now; keep the $46.99 (about $51.81 with tax) in the crash reserve and re-order one motor from SunnySky USA the day a motor is damaged. Revisit the spare in early March, once the thrust-stand and hover-tether tests have used the motors.

**Confidence:** medium. The cost side is verified from the repo. The vendor lead time could not be checked (the SunnySky page returned HTTP 429), so the replacement time is a judgement.

**Depends on:**
- D10 and the BOM regeneration. I assume the 11-02 BOM is the base ($145.45 reserve, 5 X4110S: 4 lift + 1 pusher).
- D5-D8 (other spend decisions). If they cut the reserve below about $120, this decision stays "no spare". If they add reserve (for example a savings decision frees $50+), flip to "buy the spare".
- D1/D2 only matter through the motor count, which stays at 5.

**Reasoning:**
- Verified facts
  - The 11-02 BOM has 5 X4110S and no spare line (`docs/purchasing/C0_BOM.csv`, last two SunnySky lines). The spare was removed on 2026-11-01 and "held as reserve cash" (`docs/DESIGN-BASIS.md` line 72). v0.3.1 carried it at line 41 of `inventory/v031/docs/purchasing/C0_BOM.csv`.
  - The pusher uses the same motor, so one spare would cover all 5 positions (BOM note, `docs/research/budget-lift.md` A3 row).
  - ESCs and props are already spared at no extra cost. LANRC 2 x 4-pack gives 8 ESCs for 5 positions (3 spare). JeeFly gives 6 lift props for 4 positions, and APC pusher props give 3 for 1. The motor is the only unspared drive item, so no other spare is a better use of the money.
  - The reserve is $145.45 (`analysis/results.json`; BOM lines 53-57). Adding the spare costs $46.99 x 1.1025 = $51.81 (shipping is free either way because the 5-motor order is over SunnySky's $100 threshold, `docs/research/bom-savings.md` lines 37 and 97). The reserve would fall to $93.64, below the R7 target of at least $100 (`docs/DESIGN-BASIS.md` line 25).
  - Hover motor-out is not met by C0 and is accepted with hover limits of at most 5 m AGL and 60 s (R14, `docs/DESIGN-BASIS.md` lines 32 and 64). So a failed motor means a grounded aircraft, as DECISIONS.md D9 says. Cruise motor-out is a hard landing, which is a crash, not a repair.
- Cost of waiting
  - A later single replacement costs $46.99 + about $4.80 tax + shipping. Shipping is probably $5-10 because a single motor falls under the $100 free threshold. That is roughly $57-62, about $6-10 more than buying now. This is the price of the insurance if a motor fails.
  - SunnySky USA is a US shop (budget-lift.md lists "US (SunnySky USA)" and Columbus OH for its props). Judgement: a ground order arrives in about 3-7 days. Motor failures are not predictable, but the replacement delay is days, not weeks, and a flight-test campaign with several weekends in spring absorbs one lost weekend.
  - The X4110S is a catalogued SunnySky stock item, "Add to Cart" at the time of the research (budget-lift.md A3 row). The risk of it going out of stock is small but real (small shop).
- Why not buy anyway
  - A crash that kills one motor usually also costs props, a boom or foam. The reserve is meant for that, and a motor is the single largest item in it. Holding cash covers any of those, while a spare covers only one.
  - Mentor guidance is to converge and keep the BOM simple. The spare was already removed in the 11-02 baseline. Re-adding it is a reversal that needs a reason, and the reserve target is the reason against it.
  - Most motor failure risk is in early bench and thrust-stand work, where a replacement delay costs nothing because the flight test is months away. The early failure modes (a bad bearing, a shorted winding, a mount crack) show up before April.

**Risks / what would change the call:**
- If SunnySky shows long backorder or no stock when checked (the 429 hid this), buy the spare now. Check the page before the main order and look at inventory on the product page.
- If the April flight window is only one or two days (a booked field or a sponsor visit), a single failure ends the test campaign. Buy the spare in that case.
- If D10 ends with a reserve of at least $150 after D5-D8, buying the spare still leaves at least $100 and is a fair use of the money. Flip to buy.
- If the first thrust-stand test shows a hot or noisy motor (R21 thermal limits, `docs/research/thermal.md`), buy a spare and replace the doubtful unit.
- A seller price change. The $46.99 price was checked 2026-10-13.
