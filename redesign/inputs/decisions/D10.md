# D10. Committed spend and reserve

**Recommendation:** Make the root `docs/purchasing/C0_BOM.csv` (11-02, $1,854.55 committed) the single BOM, add one $25 shop-consumables line, and plan on a $100 hard floor / about $120 expected crash reserve, rising to about $165 if the hot-wire kit is borrowed. Hold the spare X4110S as earmarked cash, not a purchase.

**Confidence:** High on which BOM wins and on the arithmetic (both BOMs re-summed to the cent). Medium on the reserve target, because crash cost is a judgement and OnlineMetals shipping ($50) and the RMRC free-shipping rule are still unverified estimates.

**Depends on:** D1 (assumes the 11-02 baseline is adopted). D5 AS150U, D6 LANRC, D7 RMRC MS4525DO, D8 RMRC SAM-M10Q, D9 no spare motor are assumed as the base case; the table below gives the figure under the alternatives.

## Reasoning

### 1. Tax and shipping: both BOMs tax everything
The premise "one has tax, the other none" is not right. Both apply Berkeley 10.25 % to all goods and add a separate shipping allowance.

| | Root (11-02) | v0.3.1 |
|---|---|---|
| Goods lines (re-summed) | 50 lines, $1,598.69 | 40 lines, $1,630.77 |
| Tax = goods x 10.25 % | $163.87 (recomputed: 163.87) | $167.15 (recomputed: 167.15) |
| Shipping allowance | $91.99 | $100.00 |
| Committed | $1,854.55 | $1,897.92 |
| Reserve to $2,000 | $145.45 | $102.08 |

Verified: every line's qty x unit equals ext in both files, and the tax lines match to the cent. Shipping is untaxed in both (reasonable: separately stated carrier shipping is generally not taxable in CA; judgement, not verified with CDTFA). Fees (FAA $5, AMA $89) sit outside the cap in both (`docs/DESIGN-BASIS.md` R6).

### 2. Which is more accurate
Root BOM wins on accuracy:
- **Shipping is priced vendor by vendor.** $91.99 = $7.99 quoted Amazon + $70 EST (OnlineMetals $50, Bolt Depot $10, Neu Racing $10, RMRC $0) + 20 % contingency ($84) (`docs/research/bom-savings.md`, Shipping and Consolidation sections). The v0.3.1 $100 is a flat estimate that bom-savings.md showed to be too low for its own vendor mix ($186.94 for that mix).
- **Itemised.** The v0.3.1 "Hardware kit" line ($124.01) is a lump; the root BOM splits it into 12 priced lines plus Bolt Depot A2 fasteners ($22.38, 139 screws) and the AS150U sets ($34.52). The itemised equivalents sum to $167.25, i.e. the 11-02 BOM is about $43 richer in hardware, reflecting real needs (67 inserts, 4 extension-lead packs for the 11.8 m harness, 12 XT60H pairs).
- **Stock-checked 11-01** (FC backorder, BeeID 2 left, RP3 backorder noted in line notes). v0.3.1 prices are 10-13, with a SoloGood "only 1 left" flag.
- **Matches the baseline:** 7.438 kg, AS150U (R20), the electrical path.

What the root BOM dropped relative to v0.3.1 (this is why its reserve looks bigger): spare X4110S ($46.99), spare servo ($19.99), spare Du-Bro pushrod pack ($12.48), and the hinge-tape line (the packing tape now does hinges). These are real resilience cuts, not savings.

Where the root BOM is weak:
1. **Hot-wire kit $41.73** is flagged "borrow if a makerspace has one". If Jacobs has a bow, this is $46.01 with tax that returns to the reserve.
2. **Footer is stale:** "Prices checked 2026-10-13" while lines were re-checked 11-01 (`docs/purchasing/C0_BOM.csv` last line). Prices are now 8-27 days old.
3. **No consumables line** for items the manual itself calls a Gap: sandpaper, masking tape, paint pen, isopropyl, template backing, $10-15 EST (`docs/build/BUILD-MANUAL.md` line 118, G1 at line 1318), plus solder and flux (K4 soldering is makerspace, but solder/flux is not on any BOM). The $0 "makerspace assumed" tool list (BUILD-MANUAL lines 76-97) hides real small spend.
4. **Downstream copies are stale:** `BUILD-MANUAL.md` lines 94 and 195/199 quote "$1,854.17 / $145.83" and "51 goods lines" (BOM now 50 lines, $1,854.55 / $145.45).
5. **Both BOMs have empty "Ordered / Order date / Actual USD" columns**: nothing has been ordered, so all prices are quotes.

`results.json` has a `bomLean` variant ($1,788.41, crash $211.59, goods $1,538.70); it is not what the CSV carries, so do not quote it as the reserve.

Key points:
- The "cheaper ESC/GPS variants" are not cheaper. LANRC (already in the root BOM) is the cheap ESC; SoloGood costs +$22.03 with tax. HGLRC GPS saves $4.96 with tax but forces a second vendor shipment, a net +$1.52 worse. Sensors/GPS should follow the root BOM for the RMRC consolidation (D7, D8).

- Spare motor costs $51.81 with tax. It moves the reserve from $145 to $94.

## Risks / what would change the call
- If the hot-wire bow is not available and OnlineMetals shipping comes in above $50 (the $50 is EST; atoship.com-style over-48-in surcharge about $32 is unofficial), the reserve drops below $100. Get a real UPS quote first (bom-savings.md "Next levers").
- If RMRC does not give free shipping (rule is estimate, quoted $6.49-14.99), reserve falls by up to $15 (row above).
- If the CoreWing stays backordered and the Kakute fallback is bought, -$44.36 reserve.
- If tax-exempt purchase through UC is possible, reserve rises by up to about $166; check before ordering.
- If D9 is spare motor yes, then drop the hot-wire line (borrow) and accept a reserve near $112 (66.09 + 46.01), or buy the motor only after the first flight-readiness test.
- A hard cap that "cannot be expanded" (R6) means any crash that costs more than the reserve ends flight testing; this is the main program risk, not the BOM choice.
