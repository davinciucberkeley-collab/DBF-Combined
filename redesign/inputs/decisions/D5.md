# D5. Battery disconnect plug (R20): XT90S or Amass AS150U — SAFETY

**Recommendation:** Use the Amass AS150U (2+4 pin, anti-spark) as the single battery disconnect: one male on the aircraft trunk, one female on each Ovonic pack in place of its EC5. Retire the XT90S from the power path. On receipt, check that the anti-spark contact is present and works before first power-up.

**Confidence:** High. Both ratings come from Amass's own datasheets. The XT90S fails on hover current alone, before transition is considered. The one open item is whether the anti-spark feature is actually present on the parts we receive, and that is a 5-minute bench check.

**Depends on:** D1 (baseline) only through the current figures. The plug choice is the same on the 11-02 sizing (hover 53.9 A loaded, transition 82.1 A) and on v0.3.1 (37.5 A hover, 74.4 A transition), because the transition current is above the XT90S continuous rating on both. It would only flip back to the XT90S if hover dropped below about 35 A, which no candidate does. C1 (94.2 A transition) also fits the AS150U. Spend on D6–D9 does not affect this: the AS150U line is $34.52 and is already in the 11-02 BOM.

## Reasoning

### Manufacturer ratings (verified, Amass datasheets read for this decision)

| | XT90S-F (anti-spark) | AS150U-F |
|---|---|---|
| Rated (continuous) | **40 A** | **70 A** (main contact, 8 AWG; temperature rise test 70 A for 4 h, ΔT < 60 ℃) |
| Short-duration | 90 A "瞬时电流" (momentary), **no duration stated** | **140 A for 1 min, < 80 ℃** |
| Contact resistance | 0.56 mΩ | 0.20 mΩ |
| Recommended wire | 10 AWG | 8 AWG. Wire table at ΔT 60/85 ℃: 8 AWG 80/90 A; **10 AWG 70/80 A**; 12 AWG 40/50 A |
| Voltage | DC 500 V | DC 500 V |
| Anti-spark | Yes (it is the "S" part) | "防打火" (anti-spark) appears in the options list. It is the only option there without an empty ☐ box, so it reads as selected. This is inferred from the text extraction; see the cross-checks below |
| Mating life | 1000 cycles | 100 cycles |

Sources:
- XT90S-F: Amass spec sheet V1.2, https://sparks.gogo.co.nz/assets/_site_/downloads/XT90S_SPEC.pdf (manufacturer PDF hosted by a third party).
- AS150U-F: Amass 产品规格书 (product specification sheet), https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20220706/9099BAC3F8F6B9A2C79768E315F09F94.pdf (manufacturer PDF hosted by LCSC). It is titled as the AS150U first-generation "2+4" part for agricultural drones.
- Both PDFs were text-extracted for this decision, and the numbers match the repo's table in `docs/research/propulsion-margin-data.md:134-135`.

### Load against the ratings (11-02 sizing, `analysis/results.json` `concepts.C0.sized`)

| Case | Current | XT90S (40 A cont.) | AS150U (70 A cont. / 140 A 1 min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hover | 51.0 A | 128 % — **over** | 73 % |
| Hover, loaded | 53.9 A | 135 % — **over** | 77 % |
| Transition | 82.1 A | above continuous; within the 90 A momentary figure only by 9 %, and that figure has no stated duration | 59 % of the 1 min rating; ArduPilot transitions take seconds |
| v0.3.1 full lift throttle (`electrical-architecture.md:63`) | 133 A | — | under 140 A for 1 min |

- The XT90S is over its continuous rating for the whole hover phase, which is the longest high-current part of every flight.
- The 90 A "esk8 wiki" figure (`electrical-architecture.md:41`) is a forum number and is contradicted by Amass's own 40 A.
- The repo's existing note that the XT90S is "40 A continuous, below the hover current" (`DESIGN-BASIS.md:38`, R20) is correct.

### Anti-spark at 6S with large capacitance (judgement plus arithmetic)

- **Capacitance on the bus:** the planned bus has six 470 µF caps (FC plus five ESCs, `electrical-architecture.md:10`), plus the ESCs' onboard caps, so roughly 3–5 mF. At 25.2 V that stores about 1–1.6 J (½CV²).
- **Without pre-charge:** the inrush is limited only by pack, wire and capacitor ESR, so it reaches hundreds of amps. Each plug-in then arcs and pits the contacts, and contact resistance rises over the season.
- **Both candidates are anti-spark parts**, which is what a 6S bus with ~4 mF needs. The AS150U is anti-spark itself, so no separate loop key or pre-charge resistor is needed.
- **Evidence the AS150U is anti-spark:**
  - The Amass sheet's options box marks "防打火" (anti-spark).
  - Retailers say so: Rotorama describes "antispark technology, where the connection is first made through an integrated resistor" (https://www.rotorama.com/product/amass-as150u-1-par). Neu Racing describes an "anti-spark design" (https://neuracing.us/product/as150u/). Both are retailers, not Amass.
- **Caution:** one buyer review found by web search said a seller shipped AS150U packs with no "AS" anti-spark terminals. That is a single, unverified retail review, but it is why R20 already says "anti-spark contact confirmed on receipt", and that check should stay.

### Wire gauge and re-termination

- **BOM wire:** the main battery leads are 10 AWG (`docs/purchasing/C0_BOM.csv:12`, B0D968WN4W; `budget-airframe.md:283`). The ESC branches are 12 AWG.
- **AS150U on 10 AWG:** the solder cups are sized for 8 AWG, and Neu Racing says the set suits 12–6 AWG (retailer). Amass's wire table gives 70 A at ΔT 60 ℃ on 10 AWG, so the 10 AWG trunk does not derate the plug below hover current.
- **XT90S:** its cup is natively 10 AWG, but that does not rescue its 40 A rating.
- **Pack leads:** both BOMs re-terminate the Ovonic EC5 leads anyway (`C0_BOM.csv:32`). So the XT90S does not save a re-termination step, unless the packs were switched to the Ovonic Roam XT90-S version (`budget-energy-test.md:13`), which would bring back the under-rated plug.
- The pack lead gauge for B09CTRKLM4 is not stated in the repo, so check it on receipt. 10 or 8 AWG both fit the AS150U.

### Availability and cost

- The AS150U set (1 male + 1 female, gold bullets, shells) is $17.26 at Neu Racing, a US seller. There is no explicit stock flag, but the page has an add-to-cart button.
- The BOM already carries 2 sets for $34.52 (`C0_BOM.csv:2`). That gives 1 aircraft male, 2 pack females and 1 spare male, and the spare male can become the charge lead.
- The XT90S is already in the v0.3.1 hardware kit (`inventory/inventory.csv` P-04), so it costs $0. Keeping it saves only about $35 and leaves a known over-rating on the safety plug.

### The disconnect as the physical safety switch (R20)

- **Keying and polarity:** the AS150U is a single keyed body with 2 power pins and 4 signal pins, so it cannot be mated with reversed polarity.
- **Pull force:** 4–8 kg insertion and extraction, so it holds against vibration. It is also stiff, so mount it where it can be pulled with a gloved hand and where pulling it does not load the solder joints (the datasheet warns against powering a connector under mechanical load).
- **Fit with R20:** "plug last, pull first" (`hardware-schedule.md:50`) still works. The 4 signal pins can stay unused.
- **Mating life:** 100 cycles is low. At about 2 mates per flight plus bench work, budget for replacing the pack-side females during the season. The spare set covers one replacement.

### Alternatives considered

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| AS150 7 mm separate bullets / XT150 | No Amass sheet found (`propulsion-margin-data.md:137`). Two loose bullets give no polarity keying, which is worse for a safety plug |
| XT90H (non-anti-spark, 45 A) | Below hover current and no anti-spark |
| Parallel XT90S or an arming loop key | More parts and failure points for no gain over one AS150U |

## Risks / what would change the call
- **Counterfeit or no-resistor parts.** If the received AS150Us have no pre-charge contact, keep the AS150U for current capacity and add a pre-charge step: a 10–25 Ω resistor lead, or a short loop plug. Or reorder from another seller (Rotorama, GetFPV). Do not fall back to the XT90S.
- **Connector heating in practice.** If thrust-stand or flight data show the plug body above about 60 ℃ after a hover-heavy run, move the aircraft pigtail to 8 AWG. The connector's 70 A rating assumes 8 AWG.
- **Trunk wire is a separate issue.** 10 AWG at 53.9 A hover is above the Engineering Toolbox 52 A figure (`electrical-architecture.md:63`). This is outside D5, but the plug rating does not cover the wire. Consider 8 AWG for the pack-to-PDB trunk.
- **The XT90S could only come back** if the current drops below about 35 A (80 % of 40 A) at hover and transition stays well under 90 A. That would mean a much lighter aircraft and is not on the table.
