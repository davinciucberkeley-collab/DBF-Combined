# D6. Lift ESCs: LANRC or SoloGood

**Recommendation:** Buy the LANRC BLHeli_S 45A 4-pack x 2 (B0D2HS4Y4J, $60.00 plus $3.00 delivery); it is the same class of part as the SoloGood at $19.98 less, and the 4-pack format of individual ESCs is the right form factor for the booms.

**Confidence:** Medium-high on LANRC over SoloGood (the two are near-identical on every checkable spec, so price and stock decide it). Medium on the part itself, because both are unbranded Amazon clones with 3-6 ratings and no independent test data. I could not open either Amazon page (HTTP 503), so the specs below come from the repo's earlier reads plus search snippets of the Amazon listings.

**Depends on:** D1 baseline (assumes C0 stays 4 x X4110S 400KV, 17 in props, 6S, so the per-motor currents below hold) and D5 (battery plug and pack; assumes 6S, 25.2 V max). If D1 moves to larger props or a heavier aircraft, redo the current check in section 1. D2 cruise speed does not affect the lift ESCs.

## Reasoning

### 1. Per-motor current vs the 45 A rating (verified from repo, arithmetic mine)
Source: `analysis/results.json` concepts.C0.sized, maker table in `docs/research/budget-lift.md:42`.

| Case | Total | Per ESC | % of 45 A continuous |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hover, base (I_hover 50.99 A at 22.2 V) | 50.99 A | 12.7 A | 28 % |
| Hover, loaded pack (I_hover_loaded 53.90 A) | 53.90 A | 13.5 A | 30 % |
| Transition, 40 s (I_trans 82.06 A, mean, EST) | 82.06 A | 20.5 A | 46 % |
| One motor at full static thrust (X4110S 3130 gf at 27.7 A, 22.2 V) | n/a | 27.7 A | 62 % |
| Same at 25 V full pack (3600 gf at 33.3 A) | n/a | 33.3 A | 74 % of continuous, 61 % of the 55 A 10 s burst |

Even the worst single-motor case stays under 45 A continuous. Margin is about 1.35x at the worst case and 3.5x in hover. Both parts are listed 45 A continuous / 55 A for 10 s (`docs/research/propulsion-margin-data.md:95`, `docs/research/bom-savings.md:16`), so the current check does not separate them. The real limit is thermal, not current: ESCs sit in prop wash on the booms, and THM-05 predicts 50.3 C after 60 s of hover and 61.7 C at mission peak against a 70 C limit, which is itself an estimate (`docs/validation/VALIDATION-MATRIX.md:80`). Neither seller publishes efficiency or a temperature limit.

### 2. 6S support
Both listings state 2-6S (SoloGood listing title: "2-6S"; LANRC: "2-6S Lipo"; search snippets of the listings, https://www.amazon.com/clp/B0D2HS4Y4J and https://www.amazon.com/SoloGood-Brushless-Electronic-Controller-Quadcopter/dp/B0DJQVGSBC, retailer pages, not maker data). Caution: Amazon also carries a SoloGood 35A version listed "2-5S" (B0DJQY1HLS), so the voltage range differs by SKU. Check the ASIN before ordering. Pack max is 25.2 V, and the 470 uF 35 V capacitor at each ESC in `docs/research/electrical-architecture.md:10` is what protects against regen spikes on a part whose FET voltage rating is unpublished. Keep that capacitor requirement regardless of brand.

### 3. DShot / BLHeli_S and risk R-A
- Both are BLHeli_S with DShot 150/300/600. The EFM8BB21F16G MCU is quoted on a related clone listing, not on either of ours (listing text from search snippets; the repo has "ESC MCU: —", `firmware/peripherals.md:11`).
- The ArduPilot config in `firmware/vtol_c0.param:62-66` (`Q_M_PWM_TYPE,4` = DShot150, `SERVO_BLH_MASK,256`, `SERVO_BLH_OTYPE,4`, `SERVO_BLH_AUTO,1`) needs only DShot150 and BLHeli_S passthrough. Both parts meet that equally. ArduPilot recommends DShot150 for larger aircraft with long signal leads (https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/common-dshot-escs.html, maker docs).
- Risk R-A (`electrical-architecture.md:38`) is about the FC output (function 70 on S9 via `SERVO_BLH_MASK`, forum-sourced), not about the ESC brand. Choosing SoloGood would not reduce it. The ArduPilot DShot page does not mention `SERVO_BLH_MASK` or function 70, which supports keeping the bench check (`electrical-architecture.md:85`) and the PWM + calibration fallback. The fallback also works on both ESCs, since BLHeli_S accepts standard PWM.
- Not documented by either seller: factory BLHeli_S revision, and whether the ESCs have a BEC (not needed; the FC powers servos via the UBEC branch).
- The ArduPilot page lists passthrough and telemetry as BLHeli32/AM32 features, while the repo's setup relies on BLHeli_S passthrough via ESC Configurator. `firmware/peripherals.md:1.1` already has a fallback (USB linker). Bench-verify on whichever ESC arrives. This is identical for both options.

### 4. Form factor
Both are packs of four individual ESCs (SoloGood title "4Pcs 45A"; LANRC "4PCS 45A ESC"), about 45 x 25 x 8 mm, 6-6.6 g, 18 AWG leads (`bom-savings.md:16`, CAD placeholder `fusion/variants/build_a1_fusion.py:548`). That is what C0 needs: one ESC at each boom-mounted motor (2 per boom), short phase leads, one ESC in the pod for the pusher, and a 8-ESC buy gives 3 spares. A 4-in-1 board (about $20-30 for 45A, e.g. https://www.amazon.com/SPARKHOBBY-Controller-Support-BLHeli-S-DSHOT600/dp/B0D1QTQWXX) is built for a 30.5 mm stack in a quad frame, so it would force long motor leads down two 1.5 m booms or a central ESC with 4 long phase runs. It is also a single point of failure. Reject 4-in-1. (Judgement, not tested.)

### 5. Legitimacy, reviews, availability
- LANRC listing (seller Sparkhobby): 3 ratings, "15 left" at 2026-11-01, $1.50 delivery per pack, delivery Nov 13 (probably ships from China) (`docs/research/bom-savings.md:21-26`).
- SoloGood listing: 6 ratings, average 3.7 of 5 (`propulsion-margin-data.md:144`), "Only 1 left" on 2026-11-01 (`bom-savings.md:63`). The BOM note already flagged "low stock" in v0.3.1 (`inventory/v031/docs/purchasing/C0_BOM.csv:16`). An 8-ESC order cannot be filled by a listing with 1 left.
- A web search found no independent hands-on test of either part. Amazon carries many near-identical 45A BLHeli_S four-packs under other names (HNYYZL, Readytosky), which suggests a common OEM design relabeled. Treat both as generic clones; neither is more trustworthy. A SoloGood storefront page (https://sologood.net) shows the 45A at $30.99 (retailer page, not checked for stock or shipping), so SoloGood's price may be negotiable, but I did not verify it.
- LANRC's 3 ratings are an acknowledged risk, but SoloGood's 3.7 average on 6 ratings is no better.

### 6. Price
LANRC 2 x $30.00 + 2 x $1.50 delivery = $63.00. SoloGood 2 x $39.99 = $79.98 (delivery not recorded). LANRC saves $19.98 in goods ($22.03 with tax per `bom-savings.md:8`) with the same specs. Reserve after the whole BOM review is $94.02 (`bom-savings.md:110`), so the saving matters.

## Risks / what would change the call
- **Quality of an unbranded part is the real risk**, for either brand. Mitigation (judgement): when the 8 ESCs arrive, bench-test all of them at 6S on the thrust stand (test T2) with the real 17 in prop before any flight. Reject any that desync, run hot, or lose DShot. The 3 spares cover this. Add a thermal check at the 70 C EST limit (`test-plan.html:1177`).
- If the LANRC listing goes out of stock or the price rises above about $40, switch to SoloGood B0DJQVGSBC; the spec equivalence means no design changes. A ~$35-40 ESC from a known maker (not researched here) would be the upgrade path if the bench test fails; this would cost roughly $50+ more.
- If a LANRC unit proves 35 A-class (listings mix 35 A and 45 A SKUs; B0D2HQPNG9 is the 35A), the margin falls: 33.3 A would exceed it. Confirm the received label says 45A.
- The ESC loss fraction (4 %) and the 70 C limit in THM-05 are estimates for both parts; update after the first measured hover.
