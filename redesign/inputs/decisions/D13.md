# D13. Which CAD model is the build reference

**Recommendation:** Make the SOLIDWORKS rev G model (`cad/`, generated from `analysis/results.json` + `cad/hardware.json`) the only build reference. Freeze the Fusion model (`fusion/`, `inventory/fusion/`, `inventory/hardware/`) as a v0.3.1 record. Do not migrate it. Carry its three ideas into `cad/` as data: the joint/interface schema, control-surface throws and the motion-clearance sweep. Fusion stays useful as a free viewer and motion-check tool that imports the rev G STEP, never as a second source.

**Confidence:** High on the choice of reference. Every build document already follows rev G, and the Fusion model is two sizings behind with its own hardware IDs. Medium on the "port the ideas" part, because the effort is an estimate and the team has not said who owns `cad/` besides Dillon.

**Depends on:**
- **D1 (baseline).** Assumes the 11-02 resize (v0.4.0, MTOW 7.44 kg, chord 322.3 mm) is adopted. If D1 kept v0.3.1, the Fusion geometry would match the baseline. Even then I would regenerate `cad/` at v0.3.1 rather than build from Fusion, because only `cad/` follows the sizing automatically (see below). So the call does not flip, but the follow-up work changes.
- **D2 (cruise speed) and any later resize.** These strengthen the call: each resize regenerates `cad/` for free. Fusion needs re-embedding by hand.

## Reasoning

**1. Only `cad/` follows the sizing and drives the build documents. (Verified.)**
- `cad/README.md:3`: "Everything here is generated from `analysis/results.json` and `cad/hardware.json` ... Nothing is modelled by hand."
- `cad/params.mjs` turns the sizing into `params.txt`. `BuildVtol.cs`, `Drawings.cs` (DBF-DRW-001 rev G, 9 sheets), `BuildFigs.cs` (build-manual figures), `Sim.cs`/`Thermal.cs` and `ExportMesh.cs` (review viewer) all read that one file through `Shared.cs`.
- `params.mjs` enforces design rules at build time: the J10 bolt-length search (line 130), the F09 consistency check (line 132) and pusher/lift disc clearance ≥ 20 mm (line 340).
- The last build (`cad/README.md`, model content section) has 397 components and 75 unique parts with every fastener. It shows 263 interferences: 189 intended insert press fits plus 74 explained artefacts, and none unexplained. Mass roll-up is 6.683 kg.

**2. The Fusion model is a hand-driven port, two sizings behind, on a different hardware schedule. (Verified.)**
- `fusion/build_vtol_fusion.py:1-8` calls itself a "Port of cad/BuildVtol.cs" and embeds `params.txt` values as literals. It does not read the file: `chord 0.238675`, `fusL 0.85`, `segF 0.955` are v0.3.1. Rev G has 322.3 mm chord, 1.000 m pod and 1046.0 mm front boom (`cad/README.md`).
- The detail and joint stages read `inventory/hardware/hardware.json` "rev 2" (2026-11-01). It uses its own joint IDs (`J-01`...`J-xx`, `HW-058`...). `cad/hardware.json` and `docs/research/hardware-spec.md` §4 use `J01`–`J20` and `F01`–`F13`. These are two incompatible hardware schedules.
- That rev 2 schedule is already stale against rev G. Its `J-01` note says "NOT in the CAD: nothing retains the panel along the joiner", but rev G retains each panel with the M5 × 25 bolt pillar and root tongue (J10, `cad/README.md` wing-root bullet).
- `fusion/build_joints_fusion.py:8-10`: the document is a **direct-modelling** design, so it is not parametric.
- The motion-clearance fixes (`fusion/motion_clearance.md`: cove cuts, relief cuts, pocket extension) were applied to the B-rep and saved in the document "Motion clearance fixes". A re-run from data would not reproduce them unaided.
- The scripts run through an MCP `exec` from `C:/Users/trist/Documents/DBF-Fusion-Export/` (`README.md:212-214`), so they depend on one machine.

**3. What Fusion has that SOLIDWORKS lacks. (Verified, from the files.)**
- **Joints and motion.** `fusion/joints_spec.json` has revolute/slider joints with limits and motion links. Example: aileron hinge ±26.15°, servo arm ±45°.
- **Swept motion clearance.** `fusion/motion_clearance_report.json` has 19 servo steps per mechanism and minimum clearances.
- **A per-joint interface record.** `inventory/hardware/hardware.json` joints list `parts`, `removable`, `tool` and `assembly_order`, and `mechanisms.json` holds the mechanisms. This is close to the part-interface knowledge graph the mentor asked for.
- **Wheel gear.** It was proposed in Fusion. `team-crosscheck.md` §3 says rev G's 44.8 mm pusher clearance already meets the ≥ 30 mm requirement.

`cad/` has a static interference check only. It has no mates, joints or surface-throw sweep (grep of `BuildVtol.cs`/`params.mjs` finds none).

**4. What already crossed over.** `docs/research/team-crosscheck.md` lists X1–X5. The main CAD finding, that the battery is sealed in the pod (X1), was found in Fusion and is now fixed in rev G: access well, 225 mm bay, 220 mm tray, ray-cast verified. So the Fusion model's most important result has already paid off in SOLIDWORKS. Its other open result is the hinge-gap/pushrod collisions. Those come from v0.3.1 geometry (4.77 mm aileron gap) and must be re-checked on rev G (6.4 mm gap, BUILD-MANUAL line 522) rather than copied.

**5. Tools and people.** From the transcript (`scratchpad/transcript_clean.txt` line 42, Tristan): "I use Fusion ... my computer is, like, very slow."
- SOLIDWORKS runs on Dillon's Windows machine: SOLIDWORKS 2025 + Simulation, Educational edition (`cad/README.md` Simulations section and the line-75 licence note; `README.md:175`).
- Fusion is free for students, but it does not remove the slow-computer problem.
- Under judgement: the generator approach means nobody has to drive the CAD interactively. A teammate edits `params.mjs`, `hardware.json` or the sizing, and the build runs on the licensed machine. Without a licence, the outputs are still available to everyone: STEP, meshes, the review page and the drawing PDF.
- Bus factor is the real cost. Today only one machine can build `cad/`.

**6. Migration effort, either way. (Judgement.)**
- **Fusion → rev G:** re-embed rev G params and re-run five scripts on a slow machine. Rewrite the 15k-line rev 2 hardware schedule onto J01–J20/F01–F13. Re-do the direct-model B-rep fixes. Then keep two models in sync on every resize. That is weeks of work, and it directly contradicts "one source of truth" and "converge now".
- **Fusion ideas → `cad/`:** about 1–3 days. Add a `mechanisms` block (hinge axes, throws, servo arm geometry) to `cad/hardware.json`. Add a deflection sweep that rebuilds or rotates the surface bodies at ±max throw and runs interference. Emit an interface graph from data `cad/` already has.

**7. The mentor's knowledge graph.** The transcript (line 23 and the long mentor block) asks for every part and every connection type as nodes ("bolted", "adhesive", "wire connected"), with checks like every part connected to a joint and no overlap. `cad/` already holds most of the raw graph:
- `params.txt` `comp=` lines give the parts.
- `hardware.json` fasteners list their joint IDs.
- `hardware-spec.md` §4 J01–J20 gives the joint types.
- `verification.txt` gives the overlap check.

A generated `cad/interfaces.json`, with nodes = parts + joints and edges = part–joint–fastener, can be checked mechanically. Every comp must be in at least one joint, and every joint's fasteners must exist in the schedule. Because it is generated from the same params, it stays true on every rebuild. That works in only one direction: the graph must come from the reference model, not from a second model.

## Risks / what would change the call
- **Licence and bus factor.** If Dillon's machine or the Educational licence is lost, nobody can rebuild `cad/`. Mitigation: confirm a second licensed seat (Berkeley lab machines or a student licence; not verified here, —). If none can be had before the build, the fallback is still not the hand-edited Fusion document. It would be porting `params.mjs` → Fusion through a script that **reads** `params.txt`. That is a much bigger job, and it would flip the tool but not the principle.
- **Team buy-in.** If Tristan keeps editing the Fusion model as a design source, the repo gets a second truth again. The freeze has to be explicit, with banners in both READMEs.
- **Rev G is not motion-checked.** Until the sweep is ported, control-surface clashes at full throw on rev G are unverified. That is an open risk for the first servo-throw bench check.
- **D1 reversal** changes the work, not the reference (see Depends on).
