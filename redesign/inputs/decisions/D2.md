# D2. Cruise speed: 18 or 20 m/s

**Recommendation:** Keep 18 m/s as the design cruise, because 20 m/s fails the R10 cruise-power cap on the repo's own sizing model, and use the F08 reciprocal passes at 20 and 22 m/s to learn whether a faster setting is ever worth using.

**Confidence:** High on keeping 18 as the baseline. Medium on the gust and wind comparison, which is my own screening calculation and not a repo result.

**Depends on:** D1 (assumes the 11-02 baseline: 7.438 kg, S 0.774 m2, design stall 11.75 m/s, fixed 144.3 Wh pack) and D3 (MTOW and wing area). If D1 reverts to v0.3.1 (S 0.573 m2, wing loading 105.8 N/m2), the speeds over stall in the table change and the call should be re-run. Nothing else depends on D2 except the one-line text fixes below.

## Reasoning

**Facts (file evidence)**

- R11 allows 18-22 m/s, so both options are legal (`docs/DESIGN-BASIS.md` line 29).
- R10 needs at least 10 min of cruise. The test plan caps cruise power at **356 W**, which is the power at which the pack stops delivering 600 s of cruise after hover, transition and the 20 % reserve (`docs/test/TEST-PLAN.md` line 113). `analysis/results.json` `cruiseMarginMin` sets a 11.5 min design margin line.
- `analysis/cruise_speed_trade.json` (fixed pack, same wing, only Vc varied) gives:

| Vc | V/Vs (Vs 11.75) | CL | L/D | P cruise | I cruise | Cruise min available | vs 356 W cap | vs 11.5 min line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | 1.532 | 0.476 | 8.71 | 295 W | 13.3 A | 12.06 | pass, 61 W spare | pass, +0.56 min |
| 19 | 1.617 | 0.427 | 8.17 | 332 W | 15.0 A | 10.72 | pass, 24 W spare | **fail** |
| 20 | 1.702 | 0.385 | 7.64 | **374 W (+27 %)** | 16.9 A | **9.52** | **fail by 18 W** | **fail, and under the 10 min requirement** |
| 22 | 1.872 | 0.318 | 6.64 | 474 W | 21.3 A | 7.53 | fail | fail |

  The script's own header comment says best L/D is at 13.1 m/s (min power 10.0 m/s), so 18 is the R11 edge closest to it.
- The "-22 % cruise power vs 20" in DECISIONS.md and the param comment is consistent with this: 295/374 = 0.79. Rounded, it is -21 %.
- Today the aircraft meets R10 at 18 and does not meet it at 20 on the sizing model. Going to 20 m/s would need a bigger pack, which changes MTOW, wing and cost (D3, D5-D9). That alone is enough to decide.
- The estimate is conservative. It holds prop efficiency at 0.60. `firmware/sitl/c0_physics.py` uses the APC 14x8.5E data and gets about 0.74-0.75 at the cruise operating point. The aero build-up gives 258 W at 18 m/s (`analysis/aero/aero_results.json` `drag.cruise.P_elec_W`). Scaling that by the same 1.27 factor gives about 327 W at 20 m/s, which would pass the cap. So 20 m/s is not physically impossible. It is only unproven, and it spends all of the energy margin to buy speed the mission does not need.

**Stall margin, in ratio terms (my arithmetic, W/S 94.3 N/m2, CL_max 1.115, Vs 11.75)**

| | 18 m/s | 19 m/s | 20 m/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| V / Vs | 1.532 | 1.617 | 1.702 |
| Load factor available at stall, (V/Vs)^2 | 2.35 g | 2.61 g | 2.90 g |
| Bank angle to reach stall | 64.8 deg | 67.5 deg | 69.8 deg |
| Cruise CL / CL_max | 0.43 | 0.38 | 0.35 |
| V / 1.3 Vs transition speed (15.3 m/s) | 1.18 | 1.24 | 1.31 |

- Both are comfortably above the usual 1.3 Vs floor. 18 m/s is 1.53 Vs, not marginal.
- If the CL_max margin (4.1 %) is fully consumed, true Vs rises to about 12.2 m/s (11.75 / sqrt(1 - 0.041)). 18 m/s is then 1.47 Vs and 20 m/s is 1.64 Vs. Both stay above 1.3. That is the stall-margin test (`docs/research/stall-margin.md` M1/F09), and 18 m/s still passes it.
- Gust screen (FAR 25 style discrete gust, mass ratio about 9.7, Kg 0.57, CL_alpha 5.0/rad from AVL): a 3 m/s vertical gust uses 1.00 g at 18 and 1.11 g at 20. Against 2.35 g and 2.90 g available, both have about 0.35 g and 0.8 g spare. A 4.5 m/s gust takes 18 m/s to 2.50 g, above its 2.35 g stall limit, while 20 m/s survives at 2.67 g against 2.90 g. So 20 m/s does tolerate a stronger vertical gust. The R16 limit is a gust of 8 m/s total in wind of 5 m/s, and the first-flight cards are much tighter (steady 3-4 m/s, gusts 5-6 m/s, `docs/test/cards/FTC-F01.md` line 9 and `FTC-F04.md` line 9). At those card limits 18 m/s has margin. This is judgement from a screening formula, not a repo result.
- Wind arithmetic: at 18 m/s with a 5 m/s headwind, groundspeed is 13 m/s (still 1.1 Vs groundspeed does not matter, airspeed does), and with the wind, 23 m/s. A horizontal lull of 8 m/s takes airspeed to 10 m/s at 18 (0.85 Vs, stalled) and 12 m/s at 20 (1.02 Vs). A step lull of that size is unrealistic over the airframe's inertia and the ArduPilot airspeed controller. It does say that gusty days favour speed, and the card wind limits should be respected.

**Endurance and RTL**

- 18 m/s gives 12.06 min of cruise, 0.56 min above the 11.5 min margin line, and 2.06 min above R10. At 20 m/s the same pack gives 9.5 min, failing R10 on the sizing model.
- Range for a 600 s cruise is 10.8 km at 18 and 12.0 km at 20, far beyond the 300 m fence and the field. Range is irrelevant. Power and time are not.
- `RTL_RADIUS 100` (`firmware/vtol_c0.param` line 133) is the 2 m/s2 stopping distance at 20 m/s (100 m, zero spare), and 81 m at 18. Staying at 18 gives 19 m of spare. SITL transition took 15 s / 166 m at 18 versus 13 s / 144 m at 20 (`docs/test/TEST-PLAN.md` line 444), so 18 m/s lengthens the straight-line run by about 22 m. The turn-at-once rule already covers it. Both fit inside the 300 m fence circle with that rule.
- AIRSPEED_MIN is 16 m/s (`vtol_c0.param` line 114), so 18 m/s is only 2 m/s above the end of transition. That is thin on speed headroom in a gust, but Q_ASSIST at 13 m/s sits under it. 20 m/s would buy 4 m/s. This is the one real point for 20, and it is a firmware choice, not a reason to burn 27 % more power.

**Pusher prop design point**

- I interpolated the APC 14x8.5E table already in the repo (`c0_physics.py` lines 56-63; APC file cited there) at the sizing drag. Peak propeller efficiency is 0.748 at J 0.52.
  - 18 m/s: J 0.51, about 6,000 rpm, eta 0.746, shaft 202 W at the sizing drag (8.38 N).
  - 20 m/s: J 0.52, about 6,500 rpm, eta 0.748, shaft 255 W at 9.55 N.
  - 22 m/s: J 0.52, about 7,100 rpm, eta 0.749.
- A 14x8.5 sits at peak efficiency for any speed in the R11 band, because RPM adjusts. So the pusher choice does not decide D2. The 18 m/s point needs less RPM and lower motor power, which is easier on the 615 W X4110S. `docs/research/nautical-wings-props.md` line 29 ("Cruise at 20 m/s", the prop-designer design point) is simply stale and should say 18.
- The APC efficiency is manufacturer data at 7,000 rpm that the repo copied. It is unverified for this motor and this pusher installation (no vendor thrust table, `docs/research/pusher-battery-servo.md` section 1b).

**Surprise.** The test-plan R11 verification row says "Trimmed cruise at 20 +- 2 m/s" (`docs/test/TEST-PLAN.md` line 114), which is a leftover from before the 10-27 switch. With an 18 design that range is 18-22 and includes the design point only at its lower edge. Fix to 18 (+- 2).

## Risks / what would change the call

- If the pack grows (a different or larger battery, for example 177.6 Wh listed in `analysis/results.json` `packs`), 20 m/s could pass R10. Then 20 is a possible option for gusty days, but no other requirement asks for it.
- If the measured stall is above 12.3 m/s (R11 `AIRSPEED_MIN` check) or flights show poor gust handling at 18 m/s, set `AIRSPEED_CRUISE` to 19 or 20 for those days. This is a one-line parameter change and needs no redesign. The wing is stall-sized and the pack is fixed, so the airframe does not change.
- If F08 shows real cruise power is lower than predicted (the 0.74 prop efficiency case), the R10 argument against 20 weakens, but the stall/gust margin and RTL arguments for 18 as the default stay. Then 20 becomes an on-the-day option, not a baseline.
- If D1 selects v0.3.1 (higher wing loading, S 0.573 m2), V/Vs at 18 m/s falls and 20 m/s becomes more attractive. The re-run should use `analysis/cruise_speed_trade.mjs` on that parameter set (not run by me).
