# D11. Thermal design ambient (R21)

**Recommendation:** Keep R21 at 25 °C as a field operating limit; do not redesign for 35 °C. Fly transition and full-mission flights at Richmond Field Station in the morning, build the bay vents already in BUILD-MANUAL 8.2a, cap the take-off hover at ≤ 30 s on F06–F11/F13, and re-derive the ceiling from the C08, G03 and C11 bench measurements.

**Confidence:** High that 25 °C is workable for the schedule. The only transition site, RFS, is on the bay shore, with an April normal high of 20 °C and a May normal high of 21 °C. A 35 °C design would mean cutting the predicted lift-motor and pusher losses by roughly 10 K worth, and those losses are still estimates. Medium on the exact ceiling, which could move either way once C08 measures the real motor loss.

**Depends on:**
- D1 (baseline). I assumed the 11-02 C0 baseline: X4110S lift and pusher, 7.44 kg. A heavier or different-motor baseline would need a thermal re-run.
- D2 (cruise speed). I assumed 18 m/s. A faster cruise raises the pusher loss, which is already the tightest margin.
- Flying site. I assumed RFS for F06–F13, as R19 and TEST-PLAN §7 require. If the team moved transitions to an inland field (Livermore or Concord area), the call would get closer but would still be "accept + morning", because inland May highs are 25–26 °C.

**Reasoning:**

1. **What limits, and by how much.** All model temperatures move 1 K per 1 K of ambient (`docs/research/thermal.md` line 7 and line 84, linear model). Peaks come from `cad/sim/thermal/results.txt`; limits come from thermal.md lines 9–26.

   | Component (case) | Peak at 25 °C | Limit | Margin at 25 °C | Margin at 35 °C |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Pusher can (600 s cruise) | 76.0 °C (`pusher_endcruise_Motor`) | 80 °C EST | **+4 K** | **−6 K** |
   | Lift motor can (end of hover + transition) | 75.2 °C (`lift_mission_max_Motor`) | 80 °C EST | +5 K | −5 K |
   | Pack (sealed bay, 6.0 mΩ/cell) | 53.2 °C (`pod_mission_max_Battery`) | 60 °C | +7 K | −3 K |
   | Lift ESC | 61.7 °C (`lift_mission_max_ESC`) | 70 °C EST | +8 K | −2 K |
   | FC board | 60.3 °C (`pod_mission_max_FC`) | 85 °C | +25 K | +15 K |

   The pusher can is the tightest at +4 K, with the lift motor close behind. At 35 °C, four of the five components fail. Both motor margins rest on estimated losses: 20 % of input for the lift motor, and 33.2 W stacked on η 0.85 × 75 % for the pusher (thermal.md lines 124 and 136). The 80 °C can limit is itself an estimate, because SunnySky publishes none (thermal.md line 91).

2. **Climate at the field.** These are NOAA 1991–2020 normals as tabulated by currentresults.com, an aggregator, not NOAA itself. Checked 2026-11-09:
   - April / May normal highs: Richmond 20 / 21 °C, Berkeley 20 / 21 °C, Oakland 19 / 20 °C.
   - Inland: Concord 22 / 26 °C, Livermore 21 / 25 °C, Antioch 22 / 26 °C.
   - Sources: https://www.currentresults.com/Weather/California/city-temperature-april.php and https://www.currentresults.com/Weather/California/city-temperature-may.php.

   RFS (Richmond) is the only site that supports transitions (`docs/DESIGN-BASIS.md` R19; `docs/test/TEST-PLAN.md` line 96). So the relevant climate is the coastal one, 5 K under the ceiling on an average April afternoon. Inland sites would sit at or above 25 °C by mid-afternoon in May.

   Exceedance is my estimate, not a sourced figure. Coastal days above 25 °C in April come with offshore-wind heat events, likely a few days a month. Morning temperatures (lows of about 10 °C, mid-morning about 15–18 °C) leave about 7–10 K of margin even on those days.

3. **Schedule fit.** Transition tests run 2027-04-03 → 04-17, the full mission 04-17 → 04-18, and the buffer runs to 04-30 (TEST-PLAN lines 708–710). R19 allows flying from 6 a.m. Morning slots also tend to bring the lighter winds that R16 wants (judgement). A hot day costs at most a session, and the 12-day buffer absorbs it.

4. **Why not design for 35 °C.** Getting 10 K back on both motors would take one of these:
   - a larger pusher motor, or measured losses about 40 % lower;
   - lift-unit changes (bigger motor or cooling shrouds);
   - redoing the FEA, which the mentor's "don't spend effort on sims a design change will invalidate" argues against.

   C08, G03 and F08 will measure the real losses before any flight with a transition. That is the cheap way to learn whether the margin is real.

**Cheap mitigations that widen the envelope:**
- **Short take-off hover before transition (free).** The lift-motor peak comes from a 60 s hover plus a 20 s transition. From the transient table, the rise is +18.7 K at 30 s against +34.2 K at 60 s. The transition then adds about +16 K. So a ≤ 30 s hover gives a peak of about 35 K rise, roughly 60 °C at 25 °C. That margin of about +20 K takes the lift unit out of the limiting set for F06–F11/F13. These are my calcs from `lift_hover30/60_Motor` and `lift_mission_max_Motor`. Only F12 needs the ≥ 1 min hover.
- **Battery-bay ram-air vents (BUILD-MANUAL 8.2a, already drawn in).** Touchdown drops from 53 to about 48 °C at 25 °C (thermal.md line 140, EST). The pack margin goes from +7 to about +12 K.
- **Avionics vents (Ø8 holes, BUILD-MANUAL 8.2a) and moving the ESC to the boom side or top** (thermal.md lines 143–144). These cost little and fix the FC and ESC margins.
- **Pusher (the binding item).** Measure first in G03, then the IR check after F08's legs. If the rise after 120 s is ≥ 28 K:
   - shorten the cruise legs: the can is at 63 °C after 200 s and 70 °C after 300 s from 25 °C (thermal.md line 134);
   - or fit the aluminium spider floor (thermal.md line 145).

   If the C0 flight model's 200 W cruise is right instead of the 295 W sizing value, the loss falls by about a third and the pusher margin roughly doubles (thermal.md line 136).
- **Operations:** shade the packs and aircraft until plug-in, check start temperatures by IR (TEST-PLAN §6, already in place), and allow cool-down to near ambient between sorties.

**Risks / what would change the call:**
- C08 measures a 90 s can rise above 52 K. The ceiling then drops by the TEST-PLAN C08 formula, and a ceiling near 20 °C would start to bite. If that happens, revisit the motor or prop match rather than the ambient.
- G03 or F08 shows a pusher rise ≥ 28 K at 120 s, confirming the 33 W loss. The pusher then needs the spider floor or a throttle/prop change.
- RFS approval fails and transitions move inland. Expect frequent afternoon exceedances in May, so morning-only flying becomes mandatory.
- The schedule slips into late May or June. Coastal highs stay about 21 °C, but heat events become more common.
