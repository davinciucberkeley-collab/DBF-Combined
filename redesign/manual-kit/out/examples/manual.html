Safety

Read once before the first session. Steps repeat the rule where it applies.

LiPo Battery goes in last (step 6). Charge only in a LiPo bag, never unattended. A swollen or dented pack is not used.
Props No propeller on any motor during bench work. Props go on only for a test on the stand or a tied-down airframe.
Heat and fumes Insert iron and soldering iron run at 220-350 °C: heat-proof mat, fan or extraction on.
Epoxy Nitrile gloves, ventilated room. Mix on a card, not in a cup (heat builds up in a mass of epoxy).

STEP1Set the inserts and mount the ESC20 min
WP1FUS-S01PROPOSED

Goal ESC held by 4 screws in brass inserts on the fuselage floor.

Caution Insert tip at 220 °C. Park the iron in its stand between inserts.
  1. Press 4 M3 heat-set inserts into the floor bosses with the iron at 220 °C. Stop when the insert top is flush.
  2. Let the bosses cool for 2 min. An M3 screw turned into each insert must stand upright.
  3. Place FUS-003 on the bosses, wires toward the rear.
  4. Fit 4x M3x10 SHCS with nylon washers, one drop of threadlocker on each thread. Tighten snug in a cross pattern.

STEP2Bond the spar into both wing panels30 min + 12 h wait
WP2WNG-S01PROPOSED

Goal One straight wing: both panels bonded on the spar, roots square, no twist.

Caution Gloves on for epoxy. Mix no more than 15 g at a time.
  1. Scuff WNG-003 with 120 grit over the bonded length; wipe with isopropyl.
  2. Mark the spar centre. Tape a stop 60 mm each side of it: the panel roots.
  3. Coat the bore of WNG-001 and the spar. Slide the panel on up to the left tape stop.
  4. Repeat for WNG-002 on the right.
  5. Lay the wing flat, tips weighted. Wipe off squeeze-out. Cure 12 h at >= 20 °C.

STEP3Fit the servo and hinge the aileron25 min
WP2WNG-S02PROPOSED

Goal Aileron moves its full travel on a tape hinge, driven by a centred servo.

  1. Centre WNG-005 with the servo tester before it goes in.
  2. Seat the servo in its pocket from below, lead toward the root. 2x M2x8 screws through the grommets, snug.
  3. Hold WNG-004 at full up. Run tape along the top hinge line.
  4. Fold the aileron fully down; put a second strip into the open gap (V hinge).
  5. Fit horn and pushrod; with the servo centred, set the aileron to neutral.

STEP4Fit the motors and clamp the booms under the wing30 min
WP3BMS-S01PROPOSED

Goal Both booms parallel under the spar line, a motor on the front of each.

Caution A motor screw longer than the joint stack allows reaches the windings. Measure every screw before it goes in.
  1. Measure each M3x8 against the saddle: thread in the motor base 4 mm (minimum 3 mm).
  2. Fix BMS-003 to the front of BMS-001: 4x M3x8, threadlocker, snug. Same for BMS-004 on BMS-002.
  3. Close a printed clamp round each boom under the spar line: 2x M3x20 from below, washer and nyloc.
  4. Set both booms parallel to the fuselage axis, then tighten the clamps to 0.5 N m.

WP4WNG-S03PROPOSED

STEP6Connect the leads, then strap in the battery20 min
WP4FUS-S02PROPOSED

Goal Every lead plugged and supported; battery strapped and connected last.

Stop Props off. The XT60 is the last connection you make and the first you break.
Stop Inspect the pack before it goes in: no swelling, dents or cut insulation.
  1. Plug the left and right motor leads (MR30) at the wing root. Clip each lead to the fuselage side wall.
  2. Plug the aileron servo lead (JR 3-pin). Tape it where it leaves the servo.
  3. Multimeter on continuity across the ESC's XT60 pins: it must NOT beep (no short).
  4. Lay FUS-002 on the anti-slip pad and close the strap through both floor slots.
  5. Plug the XT60 last. The ESC beeps its start-up tune.

Glossary

For a first-time builder: the 40 words this manual uses. Underlined words in the steps show these on hover.

aileron
Hinged rear part of the wing that tilts up or down to roll the aircraft.
AWG
American wire gauge: smaller number = thicker wire. 14 AWG for battery current, 26 AWG for servo signal.
bend radius
Tightest curve a wire may be bent to without damage, measured to the wire centre.
boom
A tube that carries a motor or tail away from the wing or fuselage.
calipers
Sliding gauge that measures lengths and diameters to 0.01 mm.
clearance hole
A hole slightly larger than the screw (3.4 mm for M3) so the screw passes through freely and only the nut or insert carries load.
continuity
Multimeter test (beep mode) that shows whether two points are electrically connected.
cure
Time for glue to reach full strength. Do not load the joint before the cure time is over.
epoxy
Two-part glue: resin plus hardener, mixed by the ratio on the pack. Sets by chemical reaction, not by drying.
ESC
Electronic speed controller: turns battery power into the three-phase drive a brushless motor needs.
heat-set insert
Small threaded brass sleeve pressed into plastic with a hot iron. Gives a metal thread that survives many screw cycles.
hex key
L-shaped tool for socket screws, sized across the flats of the hex (2.5 mm fits M3 SHCS).
hinge line
The line about which a control surface turns.
horn
Lever glued or screwed to a control surface; the pushrod attaches to it.
joint stack
Everything in one fastened joint, top to bottom: screw, washer, parts, insert or nut, with torque, tool and order.
JR 3-pin
Standard servo connector: signal, +5 V and ground in one plug.
LiPo
Lithium-polymer battery. Light and powerful; catches fire if punctured, over-charged or shorted. Treat with care.
MR30
Small three-pin connector for the motor phase wires, so a motor can be unplugged.
multimeter
Meter for voltage, resistance and continuity.
nyloc
A nut with a nylon ring that grips the thread so it cannot shake loose. Use once or twice, then replace.
outrunner
Brushless motor whose outer case (the bell) spins; screws into its base must not reach the windings.
pushrod
Stiff wire or rod from servo arm to control horn.
saddle
A shaped seat that a tube or wing rests in, so it is located before the bolts go in.
scuff
Roughen a surface with sandpaper so glue grips it.
servo
Small motor with a gearbox and position control that moves a control surface through a horn and pushrod.
servo tester
Hand-held box that powers a servo and sets its position with a knob; used to centre servos before fitting.
SHCS
Socket head cap screw: a machine screw with a round head and a hex socket. Turned with a hex key.
snug
Tight by hand with a short tool, without forcing: the parts stop moving and the screw just stops turning.
spanner
Open or ring tool that holds a nut, sized across the flats (5.5 mm for M3, 7 mm for M4).
spar
The main beam running through the wing that carries the lift load; here a carbon tube.
squeeze-out
Glue pushed out of a joint as parts close; wipe it off while wet.
strain relief
Tie, tape or heat-shrink that takes pulling load off a solder joint or connector.
threadlocker
Liquid glue for screw threads (medium strength, blue). Stops vibration loosening; the screw still comes out with a hand tool. Metal threads only.
throw
